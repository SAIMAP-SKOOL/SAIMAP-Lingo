# Introduction

## How to use this book

This book takes you from your first words in English to full mastery. It has **50 units** in six levels, the levels of the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR).

| Level | Units | At the end of the level you can… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 · First steps | 1–9 | introduce yourself, ask simple questions, order food and talk about your day |
| A2 · Getting by | 10–18 | talk about the past and your plans, travel and manage everyday situations |
| B1 · Threshold | 19–28 | tell stories, give opinions, imagine situations and report what people say |
| B2 · Progress | 29–37 | argue a point, speculate and understand complex texts |
| C1 · Effectiveness | 38–44 | use English flexibly in social, academic and professional life |
| C2 · Mastery | 45–50 | understand almost everything and express yourself with precision and style |

The book is written in English. Spanish appears only in the word lists and in the **Watch out!** boxes, where we compare the two languages.

Every unit has the same parts:

- **Goals**: what you will learn.
- **Dialogue** or **Text**: the new language in context. Read it aloud.
- **Words**: the key vocabulary, with a Spanish translation.
- **Grammar** (in C1 and C2, **Language**): short explanations and tables.
- **Sounds & Spelling** (A1 to B1): one pronunciation point in every unit.
- **Watch out!**: typical mistakes of Spanish speakers and false friends.
- **Exercises** and **Answers**.

## Which English?

This book teaches **British English**, the standard variety of Europe: British spelling (*colour*, *centre*, *organise*), British vocabulary (*flat*, *lift*, *holiday*) and a modern southern British pronunciation. When American English is different in an important way, a note tells you. Unit 42 looks at the Englishes of Britain and Ireland, and Unit 45 at English around the world.

Your goal is not to sound like a native speaker. Your goal is to be **easy to understand**, with any accent.

## The sounds of English

English spelling does not tell you the pronunciation. For this reason the book uses the symbols of the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) between slashes: *cat* /kæt/. You do not need to learn them all today. Come back to this table when you need it.

Three things to remember from the first day:

- Spanish has five vowel sounds. English has twenty. Long and short vowels change the meaning: *ship* /ʃɪp/ and *sheep* /ʃiːp/ are two different words.
- The most common English vowel is **/ə/** (the schwa), a weak, relaxed sound: **a**bout, teach**er**, t**o**day. It is never stressed.
- In British English the letter **r** is silent after a vowel: *car* /kɑː/, *teacher* /ˈtiːtʃə/.

# Level A2 · Getting by

At the end of this level you can talk about the past and about your experiences, make plans, compare things, give advice and manage everyday situations.

## Unit 10 · Life stories

**Goals:** tell the story of a life, ask about the past. **Grammar:** past simple (all forms), more irregular verbs, time expressions.

![](u10.png)

### Text

My grandmother Rose was born in Liverpool in 1948. She grew up in a small house near the port. She left school when she was sixteen and got a job in a shop.

In 1969 she met my grandfather at a dance. They got married two years later and moved to London. First they lived in a tiny flat, and then they bought a house. They had three children.

Rose didn't go to university when she was young, but she never stopped learning. When she retired, she began to study history. She finished her degree at the age of seventy. After that she wrote a book about her city.

| --- | --- |

| leave school | dejar el colegio |
| get a job | conseguir un trabajo |

| get married | casarse |

### Grammar

**1. Past simple: the three forms**

| Affirmative | Negative | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| She **moved** to London. | She **didn't move** to Paris. | **Did** she **move** to London? |
| They **bought** a house. | They **didn't buy** a flat. | Where **did** they **live**? |

**2. More irregular verbs**

| Verb | Past | Verb | Past |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| be born | was born | meet | met |
| grow | grew | leave | left |
| begin | began | write | wrote |
| think | thought | take | took |
| know | knew | give | gave |
| find | found | tell | told |

A complete list is in the Appendices.

**3. Time expressions**

***in** 1969 · **in** July · **on** 3 May · **at** the age of seventy · **when** she was sixteen · two years **later** · **for** ten years · **from** 1970 **to** 1985*

### Sounds & Spelling

Many irregular pasts have the vowel /ɔː/, and the letters *gh* are silent:

*bought* /bɔːt/ · *thought* /θɔːt/ · *brought* /brɔːt/ · *caught* /kɔːt/ · *taught* /tɔːt/

They rhyme with *sport*. *Saw* /sɔː/ has the same vowel.

### Watch out!

- «Nací en 1990» = *I **was** born in 1990*, not «I born» or «I am born».
- After *did* the verb never changes: *Did she **go**?*, not «Did she went?». *She didn't **buy***, not «didn't bought».
- «Conocí a mi marido en 2010» = *I **met** my husband*. *Know* is «conocer» when you already know the person.
- Years: 1948 = *nineteen forty-eight*; 2005 = *two thousand and five*; 2024 = *twenty twenty-four*.
- ***Parents*** got married, not «the fathers».

**2.** Complete with *in, on, at* or *for*.
a) I was born ___ 1995. b) We got married ___ 14 June. c) He retired ___ the age of 65. d) She lived in Leeds ___ five years.

**3.** Write questions about Rose.
a) Where ___? In Liverpool. b) When ___? In 1969. c) What ___? History.

### Answers

**1.** a) left b) didn't have c) did … grow up d) wrote e) met. **2.** a) in b) on c) at d) for. **3.** a) Where was she born? b) When did she meet her husband? c) What did she study?

## Unit 11 · Shopping

**Goals:** compare things and prices, buy clothes. **Grammar:** comparatives and superlatives, *too / enough*, *much / many*.

![](u11.png)

### Dialogue

**Assistant:** Can I help you?
**Lucía:** Yes, please. I'm looking for a jacket. How much is this one?
**Assistant:** It's eighty pounds. The blue one is cheaper, but this one is better quality.
**Lucía:** Can I try it on?
**Assistant:** Of course. The changing rooms are over there.
**Lucía:** It's too small. Have you got a bigger size?
**Assistant:** Here's a medium. It's the most popular jacket in the shop.
**Lucía:** It's perfect, but it isn't cheap enough for me! Is there a sale?
**Assistant:** Yes, twenty per cent off today.
**Lucía:** Great. I'll take it. Can I pay by card?

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| cheap / expensive | barato / caro |
| size | talla |
| small / medium / large | pequeño / mediano / grande |
| try on | probarse |
| changing room | probador |
| sale | rebajas |
| per cent off | por ciento de descuento |
| pay by card / in cash | pagar con tarjeta / en efectivo |
| receipt | recibo, tique |
| I'm looking for… | estoy buscando… |
| I'll take it | me lo llevo |
| good / better / the best | bueno / mejor / el mejor |
| bad / worse / the worst | malo / peor / el peor |
| trousers (BrE) / pants (AmE) | pantalones |
| trainers (BrE) / sneakers (AmE) | zapatillas de deporte |

### Grammar

**1. Comparatives and superlatives**

| Adjective | Comparative | Superlative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| one syllable: cheap | cheap**er** | the cheap**est** |
| ends in *e*: nice | nice**r** | the nice**st** |
| one vowel + consonant: big | big**ger** | the big**gest** |
| ends in *y*: easy | eas**ier** | the eas**iest** |
| two or more syllables: expensive | **more** expensive | **the most** expensive |
| irregular: good, bad, far | better, worse, further | the best, the worst, the furthest |

*The blue jacket is cheaper **than** the red one. · It's the best shop **in** London.* · Equal: *as cheap **as***.

**2. *too* and *enough***

*It's **too** small.* (= demasiado) · *It isn't big **enough**.* (*enough* goes after the adjective) · *I haven't got **enough** money.* (before the noun)

**3. *much, many, a lot of***

| | Countable | Uncountable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Question | How **many** shirts? | How **much** money? |
| Negative | not **many** shops | not **much** time |
| Affirmative | **a lot of** shops | **a lot of** money |

### Sounds & Spelling

The comparative ending *-er* and the word *than* are weak, with a schwa: *cheaper than* /ˈtʃiːpə ðən/, *bigger than* /ˈbɪɡə ðən/.

*Clothes* is one syllable: /kləʊðz/. Do not say «clo-thes».

### Watch out!

- Short adjectives do not take *more*: *bigger*, not «more big». Long adjectives do not take *-er*: *more expensive*, not «expensiver».
- After a superlative we use *in* with places: *the best **in** the world*, not «of the world».
- «Que» in comparisons is *than*: *cheaper **than***, not «cheaper that».
- *Large* means «grande». «Largo» is *long*.
- *Too* is negative (more than you want). «Muy caro» = *very expensive*; «demasiado caro» = *too expensive*.

**2.** Complete with *too* or *enough*.
a) These shoes are ___ big. b) I'm not tall ___. c) We haven't got ___ time. d) It's ___ expensive for me.

**3.** Complete with *much* or *many*.
a) How ___ is this coat? b) How ___ T-shirts have you got? c) There aren't ___ people in the shop. d) I haven't got ___ money.

### Answers

**1.** a) smaller, the smallest b) bigger, the biggest c) more expensive, the most expensive d) better, the best e) happier, the happiest f) worse, the worst. **2.** a) too b) enough c) enough d) too. **3.** a) much b) many c) many d) much.

## Unit 12 · Plans and arrangements

**Goals:** talk about plans, make arrangements, offer help. **Grammar:** *going to*, present continuous for the future, *will* and *shall*.

![](u12.png)

### Dialogue

**Tom:** What are you going to do in the summer?
**Lucía:** I'm going to travel around Scotland. I'm flying to Edinburgh on 2 July.
**Tom:** Lovely! Where are you staying?
**Lucía:** I've booked a hostel for the first three nights. After that I'm going to rent a car.
**Tom:** Are you going to visit the islands?
**Lucía:** I hope so. But my suitcase is so heavy…
**Tom:** I'll help you. Shall I take you to the airport?
**Lucía:** That's very kind of you. I'll buy you a present!

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| travel | viajar |
| go on holiday | irse de vacaciones |
| book | reservar |
| ticket | billete, entrada |
| single / return ticket | billete de ida / de ida y vuelta |
| flight / fly | vuelo / volar |
| stay | alojarse, quedarse |
| hostel / hotel | albergue / hotel |
| rent a car | alquilar un coche |
| suitcase / luggage | maleta / equipaje |
| passport | pasaporte |
| airport / platform | aeropuerto / andén |
| island | isla |
| I hope so | espero que sí |
| next week / next year | la semana / el año que viene |

### Grammar

**1. *be going to* + verb: plans and intentions**

*I'm **going to travel** around Scotland. · She isn't **going to rent** a car. · Are you **going to visit** the islands?*

**2. Present continuous: fixed arrangements**

With a time and a place already decided: *I'm **flying** to Edinburgh on 2 July. · We're **meeting** at six.*

**3. *will* + verb: decisions at the moment of speaking, offers and promises**

*I**'ll help** you. · I**'ll buy** you a present. · I **won't** be late.* (*won't* = will not)

**4. *Shall I…? / Shall we…?*: offers and suggestions**

***Shall I** take you to the airport? · **Shall we** go by train?*

| Use | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plan | going to | I'm going to study French. |
| Arrangement | present continuous | I'm seeing the doctor at ten. |
| Sudden decision, offer | will | It's cold. I'll close the window. |
| Suggestion | shall | Shall we have lunch? |

### Sounds & Spelling

*To* is weak before a consonant: *going to travel* /ˈɡəʊɪŋ tə ˈtrævl/. In fast speech you will hear *gonna* /ˈɡənə/, but do not write it.

*I'll* is /aɪl/, *we'll* is /wiːl/, *won't* is /wəʊnt/. Do not confuse *won't* with *want* /wɒnt/.

### Watch out!

- «¿Te ayudo?» = ***Shall I** help you?* or ***I'll** help you*, not «I help you?». English does not use the present simple for offers.
- Plans are not *will*: «Voy a estudiar» = *I'm **going to** study*, not «I will study» for something already decided.
- *Holiday* (BrE) = *vacation* (AmE). *On holiday*, not «in holidays».
- *Travel* is the verb. «Un viaje» is *a trip* or *a journey*.
- *Luggage* is uncountable: *some luggage*, not «luggages».

### Exercises

**1.** Write sentences with *going to*.
a) I / visit / Wales b) she / not / fly c) you / rent / a car / ?

**2.** Choose the best form.
a) "The phone is ringing." "*I'll / I'm going to* answer it." b) *We're meeting / We'll meet* Tom at 7; he booked the table. c) *Shall / Will* I open the window? d) Look at my diary: *I'll see / I'm seeing* the dentist on Monday.

**3.** Translate.
a) ¿Te llevo la maleta? b) Voy a reservar un hotel. c) ¿Vamos en tren?

### Answers

**1.** a) I'm going to visit Wales. b) She isn't going to fly. c) Are you going to rent a car? **2.** a) I'll b) We're meeting c) Shall d) I'm seeing. **3.** a) Shall I carry your suitcase? b) I'm going to book a hotel. c) Shall we go by train?

## Unit 13 · Have you ever…?

**Goals:** talk about experiences in your life. **Grammar:** present perfect with *ever* and *never*, *been* and *gone*.

![](u13.png)

### Dialogue

**Jack:** Have you ever been to Ireland?
**Lucía:** Yes, I have. I've been to Dublin twice.
**Jack:** Have you ever tried Irish stew?
**Lucía:** No, I haven't. But I've eaten haggis in Scotland!
**Jack:** Really? When did you eat that?
**Lucía:** Last summer, in Edinburgh. It was delicious. Have you ever eaten paella?
**Jack:** I've never been to Spain. I've seen it on TV, though.
**Lucía:** You must come. Where's Emma, by the way?
**Jack:** She's gone to the supermarket. She'll be back soon.

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| ever | alguna vez |
| never | nunca |
| once / twice / three times | una vez / dos veces / tres veces |
| try | probar |
| delicious | delicioso |
| abroad | en el extranjero |
| ride a horse | montar a caballo |
| climb a mountain | subir una montaña |
| win / lose | ganar / perder |
| break | romper |
| forget | olvidar |
| miss a train | perder un tren |
| by the way | por cierto |
| be back | volver, estar de vuelta |

### Grammar

**1. Present perfect: *have / has* + past participle**

| Affirmative | Negative | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I**'ve** been | I **haven't** been | **Have** you been? |
| she**'s** been | she **hasn't** been | **Has** she been? |

Regular participles are the same as the past: *visited, tried, worked*. Irregular participles must be learnt:

| Verb | Past | Participle | Verb | Past | Participle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| be | was / were | been | see | saw | seen |
| go | went | gone / been | eat | ate | eaten |
| do | did | done | break | broke | broken |
| have | had | had | win | won | won |
| meet | met | met | lose | lost | lost |

**2. When do we use it?**

For experiences in your life, when the time is **not** important or not finished: *I've been to Dublin twice. · Have you ever eaten haggis?*

For a finished time (*last summer, in 2019, yesterday*), use the past simple: *I **ate** it last summer.*

**3. *been* and *gone***

*She's **been** to the supermarket.* (She went and came back.) · *She's **gone** to the supermarket.* (She is there now.)

### Sounds & Spelling

In speech *have* and *has* are contracted: *I've* /aɪv/, *you've* /juːv/, *she's* /ʃiːz/, *haven't* /ˈhævnt/. The *h* of *have* is a soft breath, not the Spanish «j».

*Been* is often short: /bɪn/.

### Watch out!

- Do not use the present perfect with a finished time: *I **went** there in 2019*, not «I've been there in 2019». This is the most important difference from Spanish «he estado».
- *When…?* asks for a finished time, so it takes the past simple: ***When did** you go?*, not «When have you gone?».
- *Ever* is for questions. In negatives use *never*: *I've **never** been*, not «I haven't never been».
- «Perder el tren» = ***miss** the train*. «Perder las llaves» = ***lose** the keys*.
- *Try* food (probar). *Prove* means «demostrar».

### Exercises

**1.** Write the past participle.
a) see b) eat c) go d) win e) break f) visit

**2.** Present perfect or past simple?
a) I ___ (never / be) to Ireland. b) She ___ (go) to Dublin last year. c) ___ you ever ___ (ride) a horse? d) We ___ (see) that film on Saturday.

**3.** Complete with *been* or *gone*.
a) Tom isn't here. He's ___ to work. b) I've ___ to Paris three times. c) "Where's Emma?" "She's ___ shopping."

### Answers

**1.** a) seen b) eaten c) gone / been d) won e) broken f) visited. **2.** a) 've never been b) went c) Have … ridden d) saw. **3.** a) gone b) been c) gone.

## Unit 14 · Just, already, yet

**Goals:** give news, talk about things you have and haven't done, say how long. **Grammar:** present perfect with *just, already, yet, for, since*; present perfect or past simple?

![](u14.png)

### Dialogue

**Helen:** Have you done your homework yet, Emma?
**Emma:** Yes, Mum. I've already finished it.
**Helen:** And have you tidied your room?
**Emma:** Not yet. I've just made my bed, though.
**Helen:** Good. Your father has just phoned. He's bought a new car!
**Emma:** Really? He's had the old one for fifteen years.
**Helen:** I know. He's wanted a new one since last summer. Have you done the washing-up?
**Emma:** Jack did it an hour ago. Can I go out now?

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| just | acabar de |
| already | ya |
| yet | ya (pregunta), todavía (negación) |
| for | durante, desde hace |
| since | desde |
| do the housework | hacer las tareas de casa |
| do the washing-up | fregar los platos |
| do the shopping | hacer la compra |
| do your homework | hacer los deberes |
| make the bed | hacer la cama |
| make dinner | hacer la cena |
| make a mistake | cometer un error |
| tidy | ordenar, recoger |
| How long…? | ¿Cuánto tiempo…? |

### Grammar

**1. *just, already, yet***

| Word | Meaning | Position | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| just | a moment ago | before the participle | I've **just** made my bed. |
| already | before now, sooner than expected | before the participle | I've **already** finished. |
| yet | until now | at the end; questions and negatives | Have you finished **yet**? I haven't finished **yet**. |

**2. *for* and *since***

*for* + a period: *for fifteen years, for two hours, for a long time*.
*since* + a point in time: *since 2010, since Monday, since last summer*.

*How long **have** you **lived** here?* — *I've lived here **for** three years / **since** 2022.* (And I still live here.)

**3. Present perfect or past simple?**

| Present perfect | Past simple |
| --- | --- |
| a connection with now; time not finished or not said | a finished time |
| *He's bought a new car.* (news) | *He bought it yesterday.* |
| *I've lived here for three years.* (I still do) | *I lived there for three years.* (not now) |
| just, already, yet, ever, never, for, since | yesterday, ago, last week, in 2019, when |

### Sounds & Spelling

The vowel /ɜː/ is long and relaxed, with the mouth almost closed. It has many spellings:

*w**or**k* · *h**ear**d* · *g**ir**l* · *f**ir**st* · *T**ur**key* · *h**er*** · *w**or**ld*

The *r* is silent. *Work* /wɜːk/ and *walk* /wɔːk/ are different words.

### Watch out!

- In Spain people say «Esta mañana he desayunado». If the morning is finished, English uses the past simple: *I **had** breakfast this morning*.
- «Llevo tres años aquí» = *I**'ve been** here **for** three years*, not «I am here since three years».
- «Acabo de llegar» = *I've **just** arrived*.
- ***Make*** and ***do***: *do* is for work and activities (*do the housework, do homework*); *make* is for creating something (*make dinner, make a cake, make a mistake*).
- American English often uses the past simple here: *Did you eat yet?* British English prefers *Have you eaten yet?*

### Exercises

**1.** Put the word in the correct place.
a) I've finished my homework. (already) b) Have you tidied your room? (yet) c) She's phoned. (just) d) We haven't had lunch. (yet)

**2.** Complete with *for* or *since*.
a) ___ two weeks b) ___ 2015 c) ___ Monday d) ___ a long time e) ___ I was a child

**3.** Present perfect or past simple?
a) I ___ (live) in Bristol since 2020. b) He ___ (buy) the car last week. c) ___ you ___ (do) the shopping yet? d) We ___ (have) breakfast at eight this morning.

**4.** Complete with *make* or *do*.
a) ___ the washing-up b) ___ a mistake c) ___ homework d) ___ the bed

### Answers

**1.** a) I've already finished my homework. b) Have you tidied your room yet? c) She's just phoned. d) We haven't had lunch yet. **2.** a) for b) since c) since d) for e) since. **3.** a) 've lived b) bought c) Have … done d) had. **4.** a) do b) make c) do d) make.

## Unit 15 · What was happening?

**Goals:** describe an incident, say what was happening at a moment in the past. **Grammar:** past continuous and past simple, *when* and *while*.

![](u15.png)

### Text

It happened last Friday. It was raining and I was cycling home from work. I wasn't going fast because the road was wet.

While I was waiting at the traffic lights, a dog ran into the road. A car was coming. The driver braked and turned left, and the car hit a lamp post. I was looking at the dog when I heard the noise.

Luckily, nobody was hurt. When the police arrived, the driver was sitting on the pavement and the dog was licking his hand. I gave my name to the police officer and then I walked home. I didn't feel like cycling.

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| happen | pasar, ocurrir |
| accident | accidente |
| cycle | ir en bici |
| wait | esperar |
| brake | frenar |
| hit | chocar con, golpear |
| hurt | herido; hacer daño |
| fall (fell) | caerse |
| noise | ruido |
| pavement | acera |
| lamp post | farola |
| wet / dry | mojado / seco |
| luckily | por suerte |
| suddenly | de repente |
| while | mientras |

### Grammar

**1. Past continuous: *was / were* + verb + *-ing***

| Affirmative | Negative | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I / he / she **was** waiting | I **wasn't** waiting | **Was** she waiting? |
| you / we / they **were** waiting | they **weren't** waiting | **Were** they waiting? |

**2. Past continuous and past simple together**

The past continuous is the background, a long action in progress. The past simple is a short action that happens in the middle.

| Structure | Example |
| --- | --- |
| past continuous + *when* + past simple | I **was looking** at the dog **when** I **heard** the noise. |
| *while* + past continuous, past simple | **While** I **was waiting**, a dog **ran** into the road. |
| past simple + past simple (one after the other) | The driver **braked** and **turned** left. |

**3. Descriptions**

We also use the past continuous to describe the scene at the start of a story: *It **was raining** and I **was cycling** home.*

### Sounds & Spelling

*Was* and *were* are weak in the middle of a sentence: *I was waiting* /aɪ wəz ˈweɪtɪŋ/, *they were sitting* /ðeɪ wə ˈsɪtɪŋ/.

They are strong in short answers and negatives: *Yes, I was* /wɒz/. *We weren't* /wɜːnt/.

### Watch out!

- The Spanish imperfect is not always the past continuous. Habits in the past use the past simple or *used to*: «De niño jugaba al fútbol» = *I **played** football as a child*, not «I was playing».
- *Wait* needs *for*: *I was waiting **for** the bus*, not «waiting the bus».
- *Pavement* (BrE) = *sidewalk* (AmE).
- «Pasó» = *It **happened***. «¿Qué pasó?» = *What happened?*, with no *did*.
- *Hurt* is irregular: *hurt – hurt – hurt*.

### Exercises

**1.** Complete with the past continuous.
a) It ___ (rain). b) They ___ (not wait). c) What ___ you ___ (do) at ten o'clock?

**2.** Past simple or past continuous?
a) I ___ (cook) when the phone ___ (ring). b) While we ___ (walk) home, it ___ (start) to rain. c) She ___ (fall) while she ___ (run) for the bus. d) He ___ (open) the door and ___ (go) out.

**3.** Join the sentences with *when* or *while*.
a) I was having a shower. The doorbell rang. b) Tom was driving. He saw the accident.

### Answers

**1.** a) was raining b) weren't waiting c) were … doing. **2.** a) was cooking, rang b) were walking, started c) fell, was running d) opened, went. **3.** a) I was having a shower when the doorbell rang. b) While Tom was driving, he saw the accident.

## Unit 16 · Rules and advice

**Goals:** talk about rules and obligations, give advice, describe health problems. **Grammar:** *have to, must, mustn't, don't have to, should*.

![](u16.png)

### Dialogue

**Doctor:** Good morning. What's the matter?
**Tom:** I've got a terrible headache and a sore throat. I feel awful.
**Doctor:** Have you got a temperature?
**Tom:** Yes, thirty-eight and a half.
**Doctor:** I see. It's flu. You should stay in bed and drink a lot of water. You mustn't go to work this week.
**Tom:** Do I have to take antibiotics?
**Doctor:** No, you don't have to take anything strong. Just these tablets, twice a day.
**Tom:** Should I come back?
**Doctor:** Only if you don't feel better by Friday. You must rest!

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| headache / stomach ache | dolor de cabeza / de estómago |
| a sore throat | dolor de garganta |
| a cold / flu | resfriado / gripe |
| a temperature | fiebre |
| a cough | tos |
| feel ill / better | encontrarse mal / mejor |
| tablet / medicine | pastilla / medicamento |
| prescription | receta |
| rest | descansar |
| chemist's | farmacia |
| What's the matter? | ¿Qué te pasa? |
| head / back / arm / leg | cabeza / espalda / brazo / pierna |
| hand / foot (feet) | mano / pie (pies) |
| tooth (teeth) | diente (dientes) |

### Grammar

**1. Obligation: *have to* and *must***

*You **have to** wear a seat belt.* (a rule, a law) · *I **must** phone my mother.* (I think it is necessary)

*Have to* changes like a normal verb: *She **has to** work. · **Do** I **have to** pay? · I **had to** wait.* *Must* has no past: use *had to*.

**2. *mustn't* and *don't have to***

| Form | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| mustn't | it is prohibited | You **mustn't** smoke here. |
| don't have to | it is not necessary | You **don't have to** pay. It's free. |

**3. Advice: *should* and *shouldn't***

*You **should** stay in bed. · You **shouldn't** work so much. · **Should** I come back?*

After *must*, *should* and *can* the verb has no *to*. After *have* it does: *have **to** go*.

### Sounds & Spelling

Three sounds that are easy to confuse:

| /ʃ/ | /tʃ/ | /dʒ/ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **sh**oes, **sh**ould, wa**sh** | **ch**oose, wat**ch**, mu**ch** | **j**ob, a**g**e, bri**dg**e |

/ʃ/ is the sound to ask for silence: «shhh». *Should* is /ʃʊd/: the *l* is silent. *Mustn't* is /ˈmʌsnt/: the first *t* is silent.

### Watch out!

- *Don't have to* is **not** *mustn't*. «No tienes que venir» (no hace falta) = *You **don't have to** come*. «No debes venir» (prohibido) = *You **mustn't** come*.
- Illnesses: *I**'ve got** a headache / a cold*, with *a*. But *I've got flu*.
- «Me duele la cabeza» = *I've got a headache* or *My head hurts*, not «It hurts me the head».
- *Constipated* means «estreñido». «Estoy constipado» = *I've got a cold*.
- Parts of the body take a possessive: *I broke **my** leg*, not «the leg».

### Exercises

**1.** Complete with *mustn't* or *don't have to*.
a) It's Sunday. You ___ get up early. b) You ___ use your phone in the exam. c) The museum is free. We ___ pay. d) You ___ tell anyone. It's a secret.

**2.** Give advice with *should* or *shouldn't*.
a) I'm very tired. b) I've got a toothache. c) I can't sleep at night.

**3.** Correct the mistakes.
a) You must to rest. b) She have to work on Saturdays. c) I have headache. d) He musted go to hospital.

### Answers

**1.** a) don't have to b) mustn't c) don't have to d) mustn't. **2.** (examples) a) You should go to bed early. b) You should see a dentist. c) You shouldn't drink coffee in the evening. **3.** a) You must rest. b) She has to work on Saturdays. c) I have a headache. d) He had to go to hospital.

## Unit 17 · If it rains…

**Goals:** talk about facts and about possible future situations and their results. **Grammar:** zero and first conditionals, time clauses with *when* and *as soon as*.

![](u17.png)

### Dialogue

**Emma:** Are we going to the beach tomorrow?
**Jack:** It depends on the weather. If it's sunny, we'll go. If it rains, we'll stay at home.
**Emma:** The forecast says it will be cloudy in the morning.
**Jack:** Then we'll leave after lunch. If we take the train, we won't have to park.
**Emma:** Good idea. What will we do if the train is full?
**Jack:** Don't worry. If you book now, you'll get a seat.
**Emma:** OK. I'll phone you as soon as I have the tickets.
**Jack:** And if you forget the sun cream again, you'll burn!

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| weather forecast | previsión del tiempo |
| sunny / cloudy / rainy | soleado / nublado / lluvioso |
| storm / fog | tormenta / niebla |
| temperature / degrees | temperatura / grados |
| the environment | el medio ambiente |
| recycle | reciclar |
| rubbish | basura |
| pollution | contaminación |
| save energy / water | ahorrar energía / agua |
| climate change | cambio climático |
| it depends on | depende de |
| don't worry | no te preocupes |
| as soon as | en cuanto |
| unless | a menos que |

### Grammar

**1. Zero conditional: things that are always true**

*if* + present simple, present simple: *If you **heat** ice, it **melts**. · If I **drink** coffee at night, I **don't sleep**.*

**2. First conditional: a possible future situation**

*if* + present simple, *will* + verb: *If it **rains**, we**'ll stay** at home. · If you **don't book**, you **won't get** a seat.*

| Condition | Result |
| --- | --- |
| If it's sunny, | we'll go to the beach. |
| If we take the train, | we won't have to park. |
| What will you do | if the train is full? |

The two parts can change order: *We'll stay at home **if** it rains.* (no comma)

**3. Time clauses**

After *when, as soon as, before, after* and *until* we also use the present, not *will*: *I'll phone you **as soon as** I **have** the tickets. · **When** I **get** home, I'll make dinner.*

### Sounds & Spelling

The contraction *'ll* is a «dark l». The tongue touches the top of the mouth and the sound is short: *I'll* /aɪl/, *you'll* /juːl/, *we'll* /wiːl/, *it'll* /ˈɪtl/.

*Won't* /wəʊnt/ has the vowel of *go*. *Want* /wɒnt/ has the vowel of *hot*.

### Watch out!

- No *will* after *if*: *If it **rains***, not «If it will rain». Spanish does the same («si llueve»), but be careful after *when*: «cuando llegue» = *when I **arrive***, not «when I will arrive».
- «Depende **de**» = *depends **on***.
- *Rubbish* (BrE) = *garbage* or *trash* (AmE).
- «El tiempo» is *the weather* (sun, rain) or *time* (hours). *What's the weather like?*
- *Forecast* has no plural *-s* problem, but «previsiones» = *the forecast*.

**2.** Match the halves.
a) If you don't hurry, … b) If we recycle more, … c) I'll tell him … 1) as soon as I see him. 2) you'll miss the bus. 3) there will be less rubbish.

**3.** Correct the mistakes.

### Answers

**1.** a) mix, get b) rains, won't go c) 'll be, are d) 'll call, arrive. **2.** a-2, b-3, c-1. **3.** a) If it is sunny, we'll go out. b) I'll phone you when I finish. c) It depends on the weather.

## Unit 18 · How things work

**Goals:** describe objects, processes and where things are made. **Grammar:** the passive (present and past simple), verbs with *to* or *-ing*, first phrasal verbs.

![](u18.png)

### Text

Tea is the most popular drink in Britain. More than a hundred million cups are drunk every day. But tea isn't grown in Britain. Most of it is imported from Kenya and India.

The leaves are picked by hand and then they are dried. The first tea bag was invented in the United States in about 1908. Today tea bags are made by machines, and they are sold in every supermarket.

How do you make a good cup of tea? First, fill the kettle and turn it on. Put a tea bag in a cup. When the water boils, pour it on the tea bag and wait for three minutes. Then take the bag out. Most British people want to add milk. Some enjoy drinking it with lemon.

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| make | hacer, fabricar |
| grow | cultivar |
| invent | inventar |
| build (built) | construir |
| sell (sold) | vender |
| machine | máquina |
| kettle | hervidor |
| be made of glass / wood / plastic | ser de cristal / madera / plástico |
| turn on / turn off | encender / apagar |
| plug in | enchufar |
| pick up | recoger, coger |
| put on / take off | ponerse / quitarse |
| look for | buscar |
| find out | averiguar |
| fill / pour | llenar / verter |

### Grammar

**1. The passive: *be* + past participle**

We use the passive when the action is more important than the person who does it.

| Tense | Active | Passive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present simple | Machines make tea bags. | Tea bags **are made** by machines. |
| Past simple | Someone invented the tea bag in 1908. | The tea bag **was invented** in 1908. |

Negative and question: *Tea **isn't grown** in Britain. · Where **is** it **grown**? · When **was** it **invented**?*

We say who does the action with *by*: *made **by** machines*.

**2. Verb + *to* or verb + *-ing***

| Verb + *to* + verb | Verb + *-ing* |
| --- | --- |
| want, would like, need, hope, decide, learn | enjoy, finish, like, love, hate, don't mind |
| *I want **to add** milk.* | *I enjoy **drinking** tea.* |

**3. Phrasal verbs**

A phrasal verb is a verb + a small word. The meaning is often new: *turn on* (encender), *look for* (buscar), *find out* (averiguar).

With some, the object can go in the middle: *turn **the kettle** on* = *turn on **the kettle***. A pronoun always goes in the middle: *turn **it** on*, not «turn on it».

### Sounds & Spelling

/b/ and /v/ are two different sounds in English. For /b/ close your lips. For /v/ your top teeth touch your bottom lip:

*boat* — *vote* · *berry* — *very* · *best* — *vest* · *ban* — *van*

In phrasal verbs the stress is on the second word: *turn **ON***, *take **OFF***, *find **OUT***.

### Watch out!

- «Quiero que vengas» = *I want **you to come***, not «I want that you come».
- «Se venden en todos los supermercados» = *They **are sold** in every supermarket*. English uses the passive where Spanish uses «se».
- *By* introduces the person: *written **by** Shakespeare*, not «for Shakespeare».
- The pronoun goes in the middle: *turn **it** off*, *pick **them** up*.
- *Made **of*** for a material you can see (*made of wood*); *made **from*** when it changes (*paper is made from trees*); *made **in*** for a place.

# Level B2 · Speaking with confidence

At the end of this level you can follow complex arguments, take an active part in discussions, write clear detailed texts and speak fluently enough for comfortable conversation with native speakers.

## Unit 29 · Future perfect

**Goals:** talk about what will be finished by a time in the future, discuss ambitions and deadlines. **Grammar:** future perfect and future perfect continuous, future time clauses, *be about to*, *be due to*.

![](u29.png)

### Text

By the end of this decade, I will have turned forty. If everything goes to plan, I'll have paid off my student loan and I'll have been running my own business for five years.

My partner is less optimistic. "By the time you're forty," she says, "you'll have changed your mind a dozen times." She has a point. I'm about to hand in my notice at the bank, and I'm due to sign the lease on an office next Monday, but I still haven't decided what to call the company.

What I do know is this: in ten years' time I won't be sitting in someone else's office waiting for the weekend. I'll have built something of my own. And once I've done that, I'll take a very long holiday.

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| ambition / ambitious | ambición / ambicioso |
| goal / aim | meta / objetivo |
| achieve | lograr |
| deadline | fecha límite |
| meet a deadline | cumplir un plazo |
| set up (a business) | montar (un negocio) |
| run (a business) | dirigir, llevar |
| pay off (a loan) | terminar de pagar |
| hand in your notice | presentar la dimisión |
| lease | contrato de alquiler |
| go to plan | salir según lo previsto |
| change your mind | cambiar de opinión |
| have a point | tener parte de razón |
| in ten years' time | dentro de diez años |
| a dozen | una docena |

### Grammar

**1. Future perfect: *will have* + past participle**

An action that will be complete before a point in the future.

*By 2030 I **will have turned** forty. · She **won't have finished** by Friday. · **Will** you **have paid** it off by then?*

**2. Future perfect continuous: *will have been* + *-ing***

The duration of an activity up to a point in the future: *Next June I**'ll have been running** the business for five years.*

**3. Three views of the same future moment**

| Form | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Future continuous | At ten I'll be flying. | in the middle of it |
| Future perfect | By ten I'll have landed. | already complete |
| Future perfect continuous | By ten I'll have been travelling for six hours. | duration so far |

**4. Time clauses**

After *when, once, by the time, as soon as, until* we use a present tense, not *will*. The present perfect stresses completion: *Once I**'ve done** that, I'll take a holiday. · By the time you**'re** forty…*

**5. The near and scheduled future**

*I'm **about to** hand in my notice.* (very soon) · *I'm **due to** sign the lease on Monday.* (scheduled) · *The minister **is to** visit Madrid.* (formal, official)

### Sounds & Spelling

In *will have*, both auxiliaries are weak: *I'll have paid* /aɪl əv ˈpeɪd/. In fast speech it becomes /ˈaɪləv/.

*By* in *by the time* and *by Friday* is not stressed; the stress falls on the time word.

### Watch out!

- *By* = «para, antes de» (a deadline). *Until* = «hasta» (continuing up to): *I'll have finished **by** Friday* / *I'll work **until** Friday*.
- «Dentro de diez años» = ***in** ten years* or *in ten years' time*, not «within ten years» (which means at some point before then).
- No *will* after *when* or *once*: *when I **finish***, not «when I will finish».
- «Estoy a punto de salir» = *I'm **about to** leave*.
- *Achieve* a goal, *reach* an agreement, *get* a result. *Reach* a place, not «arrive to».

**2.** Future continuous, future perfect or future perfect continuous?
a) This time next week I ___ (lie) on a beach. b) By December she ___ (work) here for ten years. c) Call at nine: we ___ (eat) by then. d) By the time he retires, he ___ (teach) for forty years.

**3.** Complete with *by* or *until*.
a) I'll be in the office ___ six. b) Please reply ___ Thursday. c) We waited ___ the rain stopped. d) ___ the time we arrived, they had gone.

### Answers

**1.** a) 'll have saved b) won't have arrived c) Will … have finished. **2.** a) 'll be lying b) 'll have been working c) 'll have eaten d) 'll have been teaching. **3.** a) until b) by c) until d) By.

## Unit 30 · Memories

**Goals:** describe memories and feelings about the past, write a personal narrative. **Grammar:** narrative tenses in depth, past perfect continuous, *remember / forget / stop / try* + *-ing* or *to*.

![](u30.png)

### Text

I remember walking into that classroom as if it were yesterday. I had been dreading the first day of term all summer. My family had just moved from Cádiz, and I didn't speak a word of English.

The teacher was writing on the board when I came in. Everyone stopped talking and stared at me. I tried to say good morning, but nothing came out. A girl with red hair, who had been watching me, moved her bag so that I could sit down. She didn't say anything. She simply pushed a pencil across the desk.

I had forgotten to bring a pencil case. I'll never forget her doing that.

We had been sitting together for a month before I managed to tell her my name properly. Thirty years on, she still pronounces it wrong, and I have stopped trying to correct her.

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| memory | recuerdo; memoria |
| remind (someone of) | recordar (a alguien algo) |
| dread | temer, tener pavor a |
| stare (at) | mirar fijamente |
| glance (at) | echar un vistazo |
| recall | recordar (formal) |
| vivid | vívido |
| vague | vago, impreciso |
| nostalgic | nostálgico |
| homesick | con morriña |
| embarrassed | avergonzado |
| relieved | aliviado |
| term | trimestre |
| as if / as though | como si |
| thirty years on | treinta años después |

### Grammar

**1. Past perfect continuous**

*had been* + *-ing*: an activity in progress up to a point in the past.

*I **had been dreading** it all summer. · We **had been sitting** together for a month before I told her my name.*

Compare: *She **had read** the letter.* (complete) / *She **had been reading** the letter.* (in progress; perhaps not finished)

**2. Verbs with *-ing* or *to* and a change of meaning**

| Verb | + *-ing* | + *to* |
| --- | --- | --- |
| remember | I remember **walking** in. (a memory) | Remember **to lock** the door. (don't forget) |
| forget | I'll never forget **seeing** it. (a memory) | I forgot **to bring** a pencil. (I didn't do it) |
| stop | Everyone stopped **talking**. (finished) | We stopped **to rest**. (in order to rest) |
| try | Try **adding** salt. (as an experiment) | I tried **to speak**. (made an effort) |
| regret | I regret **saying** that. (the past) | We regret **to inform** you… (formal news) |
| go on | He went on **talking**. (continued) | He went on **to become** a doctor. (next stage) |

**3. Verbs of perception + object + *-ing* or verb**

*I saw her **crossing** the road.* (in progress) / *I saw her **cross** the road.* (the whole action) · *I'll never forget **her doing** that.*

**4. *as if / as though***

*…**as if** it **were** yesterday.* (unreal: past form) · *He looks **as if** he **has** seen a ghost.* (possibly real)

### Sounds & Spelling

Adjectives ending in *-ed* and *-ing* follow the same pronunciation rules as verbs:

*embarrassed* /ɪmˈbærəst/ · *relieved* /rɪˈliːvd/ · *excited* /ɪkˈsaɪtɪd/ · *bored* /bɔːd/

*Tired* is one long syllable, /ˈtaɪəd/, not «ti-red». *Tired* and *tied* are different words.

### Watch out!

- *Embarrassed* means «avergonzado». «Embarazada» is *pregnant*. This is the most famous false friend in English.
- *-ed* is how you feel; *-ing* is what causes it: *I'm **bored*** (me aburro) / *I'm **boring*** (soy aburrido).
- *Remember* (yourself) / *remind* (someone else): *It **reminds** me **of** my childhood*, not «It remembers me».
- *Memory* is the faculty or one remembered thing; a *souvenir* is an object you buy.
- «Como si fuera ayer» = *as if it **were** yesterday*.

### Exercises

**1.** Past perfect simple or continuous?
a) I was out of breath because I ___ (run). b) She ___ (already / leave) when I arrived. c) They ___ (wait) for an hour before the bus came. d) He ___ (never / see) snow before.

**2.** *-ing* or *to*?
a) I'll never forget ___ (meet) the Queen. b) Did you remember ___ (buy) milk? c) He stopped ___ (smoke) last year. d) I tried ___ (open) the window, but it was stuck. e) We stopped ___ (have) a coffee on the way.

**3.** Choose the correct adjective.
a) The film was *bored / boring*. b) I felt *embarrassed / embarrassing*. c) It was a *tired / tiring* day. d) We were *surprised / surprising* by the news.

### Answers

**1.** a) had been running b) had already left c) had been waiting d) had never seen. **2.** a) meeting b) to buy c) smoking d) to open e) to have. **3.** a) boring b) embarrassed c) tiring d) surprised.

## Unit 31 · The world of work

**Goals:** describe jobs and responsibilities, write a formal email and a covering letter, take part in an interview. **Grammar:** the passive in all tenses, *have something done*, passive reporting structures.

![](u31.png)

### Text

Dear Ms Patel,

I am writing to apply for the position of Marketing Assistant, which was advertised on your website on 3 May.

I was awarded a degree in Business Studies by the University of Salamanca in 2022. Since then I have been employed by a small export company, where I am responsible for social media. Last year our online sales were increased by thirty per cent, and I was promoted to team leader.

I am said to be a quick learner, and I have been told that I work well under pressure. My English has been assessed at C1 level, and I am currently having my qualifications translated.

I would be grateful if my application could be considered. I can be contacted at any time on the number below, and I would be available to be interviewed at your convenience.

I look forward to hearing from you.

Yours sincerely,
Lucía Romero

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| apply for (a job) | solicitar (un empleo) |
| application | solicitud |
| CV (BrE) / résumé (AmE) | currículum |
| covering letter | carta de presentación |
| position / post | puesto |
| qualifications | titulación |
| skills | competencias |
| be responsible for / in charge of | ser responsable de |
| be promoted | ser ascendido |
| be made redundant | ser despedido (por recorte) |
| be sacked / fired | ser despedido (por falta) |
| resign | dimitir |
| full-time / part-time | a jornada completa / parcial |
| shift | turno |
| employer / employee | empleador / empleado |

### Grammar

**1. The passive in all tenses: the right form of *be* + past participle**

| Tense | Passive |
| --- | --- |
| Present simple | The post **is advertised** online. |
| Present continuous | My CV **is being translated**. |
| Past simple | I **was promoted** last year. |
| Past continuous | I **was being interviewed** when you rang. |
| Present perfect | My English **has been assessed**. |
| Past perfect | The job **had been filled**. |
| Future | You **will be contacted**. |
| Modal | I **can be contacted** at any time. |
| Infinitive / *-ing* | I'd like **to be interviewed**. I hate **being told** what to do. |

**2. Verbs with two objects**

Either object can become the subject. English prefers the person: *I **was awarded** a degree. · I **have been told** that I work well.*

**3. *have* (or *get*) *something done***

Someone else does it for you: *I'm **having** my qualifications **translated**. · She **had** her hair **cut**. · We need to **get** the car **repaired**.*

**4. Passive reporting structures**

*It **is said that** she is a quick learner.* = *She **is said to be** a quick learner.* · *He **is believed to have left** the country.* (earlier action) Also with *think, know, report, expect, consider*.

### Sounds & Spelling

Stress shows the difference between some nouns and verbs with the same spelling. Nouns are stressed on the first syllable, verbs on the second:

| Noun | Verb |
| --- | --- |
| an **EX**port | to ex**PORT** |
| an **IN**crease | to in**CREASE** |
| a **RE**cord | to re**CORD** |
| a **PRE**sent | to pre**SENT** |

### Watch out!

- «Me cortaron el pelo» / «Me corté el pelo (en la peluquería)» = *I **had** my hair **cut***. *I cut my hair* means you did it yourself.
- «Me dijeron que…» = *I **was told** that…* English uses the passive where Spanish uses the impersonal third person plural.
- *Apply **for*** a job, not «apply to a job». *A job* is countable; *work* is uncountable: *I'm looking for **a job / work***, not «a work».
- *Career* is your whole working life. «Carrera universitaria» is *a degree*.
- *Assist* means «ayudar». «Asistir a» is *attend*.
- Formal letters: *Dear Ms Patel … Yours sincerely* (you know the name); *Dear Sir or Madam … Yours faithfully* (you do not).

### Exercises

**1.** Rewrite in the passive.
a) They are building a new office. b) Someone has stolen my bike. c) They will announce the results tomorrow. d) You must wear a helmet. e) They gave her a prize.

**2.** Write sentences with *have something done*.
a) I / my eyes / test / yesterday. b) She / her flat / paint / at the moment. c) We / the roof / repair / next week.

**3.** Rewrite beginning with the words given.
a) People say he is very rich. He ___. b) They believe the thieves have left the country. The thieves ___. c) Everyone knows she works hard. She ___.

### Answers

**1.** a) A new office is being built. b) My bike has been stolen. c) The results will be announced tomorrow. d) A helmet must be worn. e) She was given a prize. **2.** a) I had my eyes tested yesterday. b) She is having her flat painted at the moment. c) We are having the roof repaired next week. **3.** a) He is said to be very rich. b) The thieves are believed to have left the country. c) She is known to work hard.

## Unit 32 · What if…?

**Goals:** speculate about the present and the past, negotiate and set conditions. **Grammar:** all conditionals, mixed conditionals, alternatives to *if*, *wish* and *would rather*.

![](u32.png)

### Dialogue

**Manager:** Supposing we offered you the job, when could you start?
**Lucía:** I could start in June, provided that my current employer agrees.
**Manager:** And what if they don't?
**Lucía:** Then I'd need a month. Unless they insist, I won't have to work my full notice.
**Manager:** I see. Would you be willing to travel?
**Lucía:** As long as it isn't every week, yes. If I'd wanted to stay at home, I wouldn't have applied.
**Manager:** Fair enough. If you had more experience, I'd have offered you the senior post.
**Lucía:** I'd rather start at this level and learn the business properly.
**Manager:** Good answer. I wish all our candidates thought like that.

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| provided / providing (that) | siempre que |
| as long as | siempre y cuando |
| unless | a menos que |
| supposing / suppose | suponiendo que |
| in case | por si |
| otherwise | de lo contrario |
| even if | aunque (hipotético) |
| whether or not | tanto si… como si no |
| be willing to | estar dispuesto a |
| negotiate | negociar |
| compromise | llegar a un acuerdo; concesión mutua |
| condition / terms | condición / condiciones |
| notice (period) | preaviso |
| fair enough | me parece bien |
| I'd rather | preferiría |

### Grammar

**1. The four conditionals**

| Type | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zero | present, present | If you heat ice, it melts. |
| First | present, *will* | If they agree, I'll start in June. |
| Second | past, *would* | If we offered you the job, when could you start? |
| Third | past perfect, *would have* | If I'd wanted to stay at home, I wouldn't have applied. |

**2. Mixed conditionals**

| Condition | Result | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| past | present | If I **had taken** that job, I **would be** rich now. |
| present | past | If you **had** more experience, I **would have offered** you the post. |

**3. Alternatives to *if***

*I'll come **provided that / as long as** you drive.* (only if) · ***Unless** they insist…* (if they don't) · ***Supposing** we offered you the job…* · *Take an umbrella **in case** it rains.* · *Hurry up; **otherwise** we'll be late.* · ***Even if** he apologised, I wouldn't forgive him.*

**4. *would rather* and *it's time***

*I**'d rather start** at this level.* (same subject: verb without *to*) · *I**'d rather you didn't** smoke.* (different subject: past form) · ***It's time** we **left**.*

**5. *wish* + *would*: annoyance and desire for change**

*I wish you **would** listen! · I wish it **would** stop raining.* (Not for yourself: «I wish I would».)

### Sounds & Spelling

In third conditionals, the contractions run together: *If I'd known, I'd've told you* /aɪdəv ˈtəʊld ju/. You will hear *I'd've*, *wouldn't've* and *couldn't've* constantly. You can write *I'd have*, but never «I'd of».

Sentence stress carries the contrast: *If I'd **WANTED** to stay at home, I **WOULDN'T** have applied.*

### Watch out!

- *In case* does not mean «en caso de que»: *I'll take a coat **in case** it's cold* = «por si hace frío». «En caso de que» = *if*.
- *Unless* already contains the negative: *unless it **rains***, not «unless it doesn't rain».
- «Preferiría que no lo hicieras» = *I'd rather you **didn't***, not «I'd rather you don't» or «I'd rather that you wouldn't».
- *Compromise* is an agreement where both sides give something up. «Compromiso» is *commitment* or *engagement*.
- *Experience* is uncountable for knowledge (*a lot of experience*) and countable for events (*an interesting experience*).

### Exercises

**1.** Complete with a suitable form.
a) If I ___ (speak) German, I would have got that job. b) If she had caught the plane, she ___ (be) here now. c) I'll lend you the car provided you ___ (bring) it back today. d) Unless it ___ (stop) raining, we won't go.

**2.** Choose the correct word.
a) Take your keys *in case / unless* I'm out. b) You can go *as long as / otherwise* you're back by ten. c) *Even if / Provided* I had the money, I wouldn't buy it. d) Write it down; *unless / otherwise* you'll forget.

**3.** Rewrite beginning with the words given.
a) I don't want you to tell her. I'd rather ___. b) We should go now. It's time ___. c) Please stop interrupting! I wish ___.

### Answers

**1.** a) spoke b) would be c) bring d) stops. **2.** a) in case b) as long as c) Even if d) otherwise. **3.** a) I'd rather you didn't tell her. b) It's time we went. c) I wish you would stop interrupting.

## Unit 33 · Mysteries of the past

**Goals:** speculate about past events, evaluate evidence, criticise past actions. **Grammar:** past modals of deduction and criticism: *must have, might have, can't have, should have, needn't have*.

![](u33.png)

### Text

Stonehenge, on Salisbury Plain, was built in stages between about 3000 and 2000 BC. Nobody left a written record, so we can only guess why.

The largest stones weigh over twenty tons. They must have been dragged about twenty miles, and the work must have taken hundreds of people many years. The smaller "bluestones" come from Wales, 150 miles away. They can't have been carried by the builders alone; they may have been moved on wooden sledges, or they might have been floated along the coast on rafts.

What was it for? It could have been a temple, since it is aligned with the midsummer sunrise. It may also have been a burial ground: human remains have been found there.

One thing is certain. The people who built it can't have been primitive. They must have had a remarkable knowledge of engineering, and they ought to have been given more credit by earlier historians.

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| mystery / mysterious | misterio / misterioso |
| evidence / proof | indicios / prueba |
| theory | teoría |
| remains | restos |
| burial | entierro |
| temple | templo |
| drag | arrastrar |
| weigh / weight | pesar / peso |
| ton | tonelada |
| mile | milla (1,6 km) |
| discover | descubrir |
| solve | resolver |
| suspect | sospechar; sospechoso |
| witness | testigo |
| remarkable | notable, extraordinario |

### Grammar

**1. Deduction about the past: modal + *have* + past participle**

| Modal | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **must have** | I'm sure it happened | It **must have taken** years. |
| **may / might / could have** | perhaps it happened | They **might have been floated** on rafts. |
| **can't / couldn't have** | I'm sure it didn't happen | They **can't have been** primitive. |

Continuous and passive forms: *He **must have been joking**. · They **must have been dragged**.*

**2. Criticism and regret**

*You **should have told** me. · They **ought to have been given** more credit. · You **shouldn't have said** that.*

**3. *could have*: a possibility that did not happen**

*You **could have hurt** yourself!* (but you didn't) · *I **could have gone** to university, but I chose to work.*

**4. *needn't have* and *didn't need to***

| Form | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| needn't have + participle | I did it, but it wasn't necessary | I **needn't have taken** an umbrella. It didn't rain. |
| didn't need to + verb | it wasn't necessary (so I probably didn't) | I **didn't need to take** an umbrella. It was sunny. |

### Sounds & Spelling

Past modals are pronounced as a single unit with a very weak *have*: *must have* /ˈmʌstəv/ · *might have* /ˈmaɪtəv/ · *can't have* /ˈkɑːntəv/ · *should have been* /ˈʃʊdəv bɪn/.

Silent letters in this unit: ***w**rite*, *thou**gh**t*, *g**u**ess*, *ans**w**er*, *cas**t**le*, *i**s**land*, *dou**b**t*, ***k**nowledge*.

### Watch out!

- Spanish uses the future perfect or «deber de» for deduction: «Habrán tardado años» = *It **must have taken** years*, not «It will have taken».
- *Must have* is deduction, not obligation in the past. «Tuve que ir» = *I **had to** go*; «Debió de ir» = *He **must have** gone*.
- *Could have done* (possibility) is not *was able to* (real success): *I **was able to** finish* = lo conseguí.
- A *mile* is 1.6 km. Britain still uses miles for distances, and pints for beer and milk.
- *Evidence* suggests; *proof* confirms. Both are uncountable.

### Exercises

**1.** Complete with *must have, might have* or *can't have* and the verb.
a) The ground is wet. It ___ (rain). b) She ___ (forget): I reminded her twice. c) I can't find my phone. I ___ (leave) it at work, or perhaps in the car. d) He ___ (be) very tired: he fell asleep at once.

**2.** Write a criticism with *should(n't) have*.
a) You didn't call me. b) He drove too fast. c) They didn't check the map.

**3.** *needn't have* or *didn't need to*?
a) I ___ (cook): they had already eaten, and I threw the food away. b) It was a holiday, so I ___ (get up) early, and I slept until ten. c) You ___ (buy) wine: we've got plenty. But thank you!

### Answers

**1.** a) must have rained b) can't have forgotten c) might have left d) must have been. **2.** a) You should have called me. b) He shouldn't have driven so fast. c) They should have checked the map. **3.** a) needn't have cooked b) didn't need to get up c) needn't have bought.

## Unit 34 · Spin and persuasion

**Goals:** report opinions and arguments accurately, recognise bias in the news, persuade. **Grammar:** reporting verbs and their patterns, the language of advertising and headlines.

![](u34.png)

### Text

The minister has denied misleading Parliament about the cost of the new railway. Speaking to reporters, she insisted that the figures had been accurate at the time and blamed the previous government for the delays.

The opposition has accused her of hiding the truth and has urged the Prime Minister to hold an inquiry. One backbencher threatened to resign unless the report was published in full.

Meanwhile, the company building the line has apologised for the disruption and has promised to complete the work by 2030. Its chief executive admitted that mistakes had been made but refused to say who was responsible. He advised passengers to check before travelling and warned them not to expect a normal service until the spring.

A spokesperson later claimed that the project remained "on track". Critics pointed out that this was, at best, an unfortunate choice of words.

### Grammar

**1. Reporting verbs and their patterns**

| Pattern | Verbs | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| verb + *to* + verb | agree, offer, promise, refuse, threaten | He **refused to say** who was responsible. |
| verb + person + *to* + verb | advise, ask, encourage, persuade, remind, urge, warn | He **advised passengers to check**. |
| verb + *-ing* | admit, deny, recommend, suggest | She **denied misleading** Parliament. |
| verb + preposition + *-ing* | apologise for, insist on, accuse sb of, blame sb for, congratulate sb on | It **apologised for causing** disruption. |
| verb + *that* | admit, claim, deny, explain, insist, point out | He **admitted that** mistakes had been made. |

**2. *suggest* and *recommend***

*She **suggested going** by train. · She **suggested (that) we go / went** by train.* Never «suggested us to go».

**3. The language of headlines**

Headlines drop articles and *be*, use the present for the past, and use *to* for the future.

| Headline | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| MINISTER DENIES COVER-UP | The minister has denied… |
| PM TO VISIT MADRID | The Prime Minister is going to visit… |
| THREE HURT IN BLAZE | Three people were hurt in a fire. |

Headline words: *blaze* (fire), *row* (argument), *axe* (cut), *bid* (attempt), *probe* (investigation), *vow* (promise), *quit* (resign), *back* (support).

**4. Distancing**

Careful writers avoid stating unproved things as facts: *She is **alleged to have** hidden the truth. · **According to** the company… · The project **appears to be** delayed.*

### Sounds & Spelling

Speakers stress a word to show contrast or to correct. The same sentence can carry different messages:

- ***I** didn't say he stole it.* (Someone else said it.)
- *I didn't **say** he stole it.* (I only implied it.)
- *I didn't say he **stole** it.* (He borrowed it.)

*Row* meaning «bronca» rhymes with *now* /raʊ/. *Row* meaning «fila» rhymes with *no* /rəʊ/.

### Watch out!

- *Suggest* never takes person + *to*: *He suggested **that I should** apply*, not «He suggested me to apply».
- *Deny* something / *refuse to do* something: *He **denied** it* (dijo que no era cierto) / *He **refused*** (se negó).
- *Pretend* means «fingir». «Pretender» is *intend* or *try*.
- *Argument* is usually a quarrel or a line of reasoning. «Argumento» of a film is *the plot*.
- *The news is* good: *news* is uncountable and singular. *A piece of news*, not «a new».
- *Notice* means «aviso» or «darse cuenta». «Noticia» is *news*.

a) "I'm sorry I'm late." (apologise) b) "I didn't break it." (deny) c) "You should take a taxi." (advise) d) "Let's have a break." (suggest) e) "I'll call the police!" (threaten)

a) QUEEN TO OPEN HOSPITAL b) FIRM AXES 200 JOBS c) MP QUITS IN TAX ROW

### Answers

## Unit 35 · Verb patterns

**Goals:** express purpose, preference, cause and feelings with precision. **Grammar:** gerunds and infinitives in depth, causative verbs (*make, let, get, have*), purpose clauses.

![](u35.png)

### Text

Learning to cook was the best decision I ever made. My mother never let me help in the kitchen, so when I left home I couldn't even boil an egg. I was used to eating ready meals, and I avoided inviting people round.

Then a flatmate made me watch her prepare a curry. She got me to chop the onions, which made me cry, and she had me stir the sauce for half an hour. I expected to hate it. Instead, I found myself enjoying it.

It's no use pretending that cooking is quick. It's worth spending time on, though. Being able to feed your friends is a pleasure, and there's no point in buying expensive ingredients if you don't know what to do with them. I went on a course in order to learn the basics, and I keep a notebook so that I don't forget what works.

I can't help wondering what my mother would say. I'm looking forward to cooking for her at Christmas.

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| boil / fry / roast / bake | hervir / freír / asar / hornear |
| chop / slice / peel | picar / cortar en rodajas / pelar |
| stir | remover |
| ingredient | ingrediente |
| recipe | receta (de cocina) |
| ready meal | plato precocinado |
| avoid | evitar |
| can't help | no poder evitar |
| can't stand | no soportar |
| be worth | merecer la pena |
| it's no use / there's no point | no sirve de nada |
| feel like | apetecer |
| in order to / so as to | para, con el fin de |
| so that | para que |
| invite round | invitar a casa |

### Grammar

**1. The *-ing* form**

| Use | Example |
| --- | --- |
| as the subject | **Learning** to cook was a good decision. |
| after prepositions | I'm looking forward **to cooking**. |
| after certain verbs | avoid, enjoy, finish, keep, mind, miss, practise, risk, suggest |
| after certain expressions | can't help, can't stand, it's no use, it's worth, there's no point (in), feel like, spend time |

**2. The infinitive with *to***

| Use | Example |
| --- | --- |
| after certain verbs | afford, agree, decide, expect, hope, manage, offer, plan, refuse, seem |
| after adjectives | It's **easy to make**. I'm **pleased to meet** you. |
| after question words | I don't know **what to do**. |
| to express purpose | I went on a course **to learn** the basics. |

**3. Causative verbs**

| Verb | Pattern | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| make | + object + verb | force; cause | She **made me watch**. It **made me cry**. |
| let | + object + verb | allow | My mother never **let me help**. |
| have | + object + verb | give someone the task | She **had me stir** the sauce. |
| get | + object + *to* + verb | persuade | She **got me to chop** the onions. |

In the passive, *make* takes *to*: *I **was made to wait**.* *Let* has no passive: use *be allowed to*.

**4. Purpose**

*…**to learn** / **in order to learn** / **so as to learn** the basics.* · With a different subject or a negative: ***so that** I don't forget · **so as not to** forget*.

### Sounds & Spelling

*To* before a verb is nearly always weak: *to learn* /tə ˈlɜːn/, *to eat* /tu ˈiːt/. It is strong only at the end of a sentence: *I'd love to* /tuː/.

Words spelt with *ch* have three possible sounds: /tʃ/ in ***ch**op*, *kit**ch**en*; /k/ in ***ch**emist*, *s**ch**ool*, *stoma**ch***; /ʃ/ in ***ch**ef*, *ma**ch**ine*.

### Watch out!

- After a preposition the verb is always *-ing*, even after *to* when it is a preposition: *I look forward **to seeing** you*, *I'm used **to eating** late*, *I object **to paying***.
- Purpose is *to* + verb, never *for* + verb: *I went **to buy** bread*, not «for buy» or «for to buy».
- «Me hizo llorar» = *It made me **cry***, with no *to*. «Déjame ayudar» = *Let me **help***.
- A *recipe* is for cooking; a *prescription* is from a doctor; a *receipt* is from a shop.
- «Merece la pena verlo» = *It's worth **seeing***.

### Exercises

**1.** *-ing* or *to*?
a) I can't afford ___ (buy) it. b) She avoided ___ (answer). c) It's no use ___ (complain). d) We managed ___ (find) a table. e) He's keen on ___ (cycle). f) I don't know where ___ (go).

**2.** Complete with the correct form of *make, let, get* or *have*.
a) My parents didn't ___ me stay out late. b) The film ___ us laugh. c) I'll ___ my assistant send you the details. d) I finally ___ him to apologise.

**3.** Join with the words in brackets.
a) I set an alarm. I didn't want to oversleep. (so as not to) b) She spoke slowly. Everyone could understand. (so that) c) He went to London. He wanted to find a job. (in order to)

### Answers

**1.** a) to buy b) answering c) complaining d) to find e) cycling f) to go. **2.** a) let b) made c) have d) got. **3.** a) I set an alarm so as not to oversleep. b) She spoke slowly so that everyone could understand. c) He went to London in order to find a job.

## Unit 36 · Phrasal verbs in action

**Goals:** understand and use common phrasal verbs naturally, recognise formal equivalents. **Grammar:** types of phrasal verb, particles and their meanings, three-part verbs.

![](u36.png)

### Dialogue

**Emma:** You'll never guess who I ran into yesterday. Dan!
**Jack:** Dan? I thought you two had fallen out.
**Emma:** We had, but we've made up. He's just split up with his girlfriend, and he's been turned down for three jobs.
**Jack:** Poor Dan. Is he getting over it?
**Emma:** He's putting up with it. He's come up with a plan to set up a café.
**Jack:** Where will he get the money? He's always running out of cash.
**Emma:** His aunt has offered to put up half. He's looking into a loan for the rest.
**Jack:** I hope he doesn't let her down. Let's look him up at the weekend.
**Emma:** Good idea. I'll bring it up when I see him.

### Words

| Phrasal verb | Spanish | Formal equivalent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| run into | encontrarse con | meet by chance |
| fall out (with) | pelearse (con) | quarrel |
| make up | reconciliarse; inventar | be reconciled; invent |
| split up | romper (una pareja) | separate |
| turn down | rechazar | reject |
| get over | superar | recover from |
| put up with | aguantar | tolerate |
| come up with | ocurrírsele a uno | devise |
| set up | montar, fundar | establish |
| run out of | quedarse sin | exhaust |
| look into | investigar | investigate |
| let down | fallar, decepcionar | disappoint |
| bring up | sacar (un tema); criar | raise |
| put off | aplazar | postpone |
| carry out | llevar a cabo | conduct |

### Grammar

**1. Four types of phrasal verb**

| Type | Example | Word order |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No object | We **fell out**. The plane **took off**. | — |
| Separable | He **turned down** the job. / He **turned** the job **down**. / He **turned it down**. | A pronoun must go in the middle. |
| Inseparable | I **ran into** Dan. / I **ran into him**. | The object always follows. |
| Three-part | I can't **put up with** the noise. / …**put up with it**. | The object always follows. |

A good dictionary shows the type: *turn sth down* (separable) / *look into sth* (inseparable).

**2. What the particles often mean**

| Particle | Idea | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| up | completion; increase | eat up, use up, speed up, turn up (the volume) |
| down | reduction; failure | slow down, cut down, break down, let down |
| off | separation; departure | set off, take off, cut off, put off |
| out | disappearing; discovering | run out, die out, find out, work out |
| on | continuation | carry on, go on, keep on |
| over | examining; recovering | think over, look over, get over |

**3. Register**

Phrasal verbs are normal in speech and informal writing. In formal writing a single verb of Latin origin is often preferred: *put off → postpone · find out → discover · go up → increase · look into → investigate*.

For Spanish speakers the formal verb is usually the easy one. The work is in learning the phrasal verb that British people really say.

### Sounds & Spelling

When the phrasal verb has no object, or when the object is a pronoun, the particle is stressed: *We fell **OUT**. · He turned it **DOWN**.*

With a noun object, the noun takes the main stress: *He turned down the **JOB**.*

Linking: a final consonant joins the next vowel, so *put up with it* sounds like /pʊˈtʌp wɪðɪt/, and *ran into* sounds like «ra-ninto».

### Watch out!

- A pronoun cannot follow a separable particle: *turn **it** down*, not «turn down it». With inseparable verbs it must follow: *look into **it***, not «look it into».
- One phrasal verb can have several meanings: *make up* = reconciliarse, inventar (una excusa), maquillarse; *put up* = alojar, aportar (dinero), subir (precios).
- Do not avoid phrasal verbs in conversation. *I can't tolerate him* sounds stiff. *I can't put up with him* sounds natural.
- *Look for* (buscar), *look after* (cuidar), *look up* (consultar; visitar), *look into* (investigar), *look forward to* (tener ganas de), *look down on* (despreciar).
- *Give up* smoking (dejar), but *give in* to pressure (ceder).

a) She turned down the offer. b) I ran into my old teacher. c) We've put off the meeting. d) I can't put up with this noise.

**2.** Replace the formal verb with a phrasal verb.
a) The police are investigating the case. b) We have exhausted our supply of paper. c) She devised a brilliant idea. d) The match was postponed. e) He has recovered from the flu.

a) The car broke ___ on the motorway. b) Carry ___ ! Don't stop. c) I need to work ___ how much I owe. d) Eat ___ your vegetables. e) We set ___ at dawn.

### Answers

**1.** a) She turned it down. b) I ran into him / her. c) We've put it off. d) I can't put up with it. **2.** a) looking into b) run out of c) came up with d) put off e) got over. **3.** a) down b) on c) out d) up e) off.

## Unit 37 · Society and the environment

**Goals:** discuss social and environmental issues, write an opinion essay, compare and contrast. **Grammar:** linkers of contrast, cause and result; quantifiers; advanced comparison; emphatic *so* and *such*.

![](u37.png)

### Text

Few issues divide opinion as sharply as the future of our cities. On the one hand, urban life offers jobs, culture and public transport. On the other hand, it is becoming more and more expensive, and neither housing nor air quality has improved as much as people hoped.

Although most councils have promised to cut emissions, hardly any have met their targets. Despite spending millions on cycle lanes, some cities have seen little change, largely owing to a lack of safe routes to schools. Nevertheless, there are signs of progress. The more people cycle, the safer the roads become. Consequently, each new lane makes the next one easier to justify.

The problem is not so much a shortage of ideas as a shortage of patience. Such changes take time, and the benefits are so gradual that voters barely notice them. Even so, both the evidence and common sense point in the same direction: the cities that invest now will be by far the most pleasant to live in.

### Grammar

**1. Contrast**

| Linker | Followed by | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| although / even though / though | clause | **Although** councils have promised… |
| despite / in spite of | noun or *-ing* | **Despite spending** millions… |
| however / nevertheless / even so | new sentence | **Nevertheless,** there are signs of progress. |
| whereas / while | clause (comparing) | Cities are noisy, **whereas** villages are quiet. |
| on the one hand… on the other hand | two sides | |

**2. Cause and result**

*because of / owing to / due to* + noun · *since / as* + clause · *therefore / consequently / as a result* (new sentence) · *so … that / such … that*

*The benefits are **so** gradual **that** voters barely notice. · It was **such a** slow process **that** people lost interest.*

**3. Quantifiers**

| Meaning | Countable | Uncountable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| not many / not much (negative idea) | **few** | **little** |
| some (positive idea) | **a few** | **a little** |
| almost none | hardly any | hardly any |

*both … and · either … or · neither … nor · each / every* (+ singular) · *all / most / none **of the***

**4. Advanced comparison**

***The more** people cycle, **the safer** the roads become. · **more and more** expensive · **by far** the most pleasant · **far / much / slightly** better · **not nearly as** good **as** · **twice as** big **as***

### Sounds & Spelling

Word families change their stress. Learn the stress with the word:

*en**VI**ronment* → *environ**MEN**tal* · *e**CO**nomy* → *eco**NO**mic* · ***PO**litics* → *po**LI**tical* → *poli**TI**cian* · ***PHO**tograph* → *pho**TO**grapher* → *photo**GRA**phic*

Unstressed vowels become schwa: *economy* /ɪˈkɒnəmi/, *economic* /ˌiːkəˈnɒmɪk/.

### Watch out!

- *Despite* is never followed by *of* or by a clause: ***Despite** the rain* / ***In spite of** the rain* / ***Although** it rained*, not «despite of» or «despite it rained».
- *Few* and *little* without *a* are negative: *I have **few** friends* (casi ninguno) / *I have **a few** friends* (algunos).
- *People* is plural: *People **are***. *Everybody* is singular: *Everybody **is***. *The police **are***.
- *Sensible* means «sensato». «Sensible» is *sensitive*. *Sympathetic* means «comprensivo»; «simpático» is *nice* or *friendly*.
- *Economic* refers to the economy; *economical* means it saves money.
- «Cada vez más caro» = ***more and more** expensive*, not «each time more expensive».

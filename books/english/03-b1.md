# Level B1 · Finding your voice

At the end of this level you can deal with most situations when travelling, tell stories, give opinions with reasons, talk about hopes and regrets, and report what other people said.

## Unit 19 · Getting to know you

**Goals:** start and keep a conversation going, show interest, ask polite questions. **Grammar:** question forms revisited, subject questions, indirect questions, question tags.

![](u19.png)

### Dialogue

**Priya:** Hi, I'm Priya. You're new here, aren't you?
**Lucía:** Yes, I started on Monday. I'm Lucía.
**Priya:** Nice to meet you. Who interviewed you?
**Lucía:** Mr Clarke did. He was very friendly.
**Priya:** He is, isn't he? So, what do you do in your free time?
**Lucía:** I'm into climbing. And I've just taken up the guitar.
**Priya:** Have you? I play the bass! Do you know if there's a music shop near here?
**Lucía:** I've no idea. Could you tell me where the canteen is, by the way?
**Priya:** Of course. It's on the second floor. We could have lunch together, couldn't we?
**Lucía:** I'd love to.

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| be into (something) | gustarle mucho (algo) |
| be keen on | ser aficionado a |
| take up (a hobby) | empezar (una afición) |
| give up | dejar, abandonar |
| get on (well) with | llevarse (bien) con |
| have a lot in common | tener mucho en común |
| colleague | compañero de trabajo |
| flatmate | compañero de piso |
| acquaintance | conocido |
| outgoing / shy | extrovertido / tímido |
| reliable | fiable, formal |
| easy-going | tranquilo, de trato fácil |
| I've no idea | no tengo ni idea |
| How come? | ¿Y eso? ¿Cómo es que…? |
| What's he like? | ¿Cómo es (él)? |

### Grammar

**1. Object questions and subject questions**

| Type | Example | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Object: auxiliary needed | Who **did** you **interview**? | I interviewed *Lucía*. |
| Subject: no auxiliary | Who **interviewed** you? | *Mr Clarke* interviewed me. |
| Subject | What **happened**? Which bus **goes** to the centre? | |

**2. Indirect questions**

After a polite opening the word order is the same as in a statement, with no *do / does / did*.

| Direct | Indirect |
| --- | --- |
| Where **is the canteen**? | Could you tell me where **the canteen is**? |
| What time **does it open**? | Do you know what time **it opens**? |
| **Is there** a shop near here? | Do you know **if there is** a shop near here? |

Openings: *Could you tell me… · Do you know… · I wonder… · I'd like to know… · Have you any idea…*

**3. Question tags**

A positive sentence takes a negative tag, and a negative sentence takes a positive tag. Use the same auxiliary.

*You're new, **aren't you**? · He doesn't smoke, **does he**? · We could have lunch, **couldn't we**? · She's been there, **hasn't she**?* · Note: *I'm late, **aren't I**?*

**4. Reply questions to show interest**

*"I play the bass." "**Do you**?" · "I've taken up the guitar." "**Have you**?"*

### Sounds & Spelling

Intonation changes the meaning of a question tag.

- The voice goes **down** when you are sure and only want agreement: *It's cold, isn't it?* ↘
- The voice goes **up** when it is a real question: *You're from Spain, aren't you?* ↗

*Wh-* questions normally fall at the end: *Where do you live?* ↘ Yes/no questions normally rise: *Do you live here?* ↗

### Watch out!

- Spanish has one tag, «¿verdad?» or «¿no?». English needs the right auxiliary each time; «…, no?» sounds foreign and «…, isn't it?» for everything is wrong: *They live here, **don't they**?*
- Indirect questions keep statement order: *Could you tell me where the station **is**?*, not «where is the station».
- *What is he like?* asks for a description (¿cómo es?). *What does he like?* asks about his tastes. *How is he?* asks about his health.
- «Conocer gente» = ***meet** people*. «Compañero» is *colleague* (work), *classmate* (school), *flatmate* (flat).
- *I'm **interested in*** music, not «interested on» or «I'm interesting».

### Exercises

**1.** Write the question for the words in italics.
a) *Emma* phoned me. b) I phoned *Emma*. c) *A dog* ran into the road. d) She saw *a dog*.

**2.** Make indirect questions.
a) Where's the toilet? (Could you tell me…) b) Does this train stop at York? (Do you know…) c) How much did it cost? (I wonder…) d) What time does the bank close? (Do you know…)

**3.** Add a question tag.
a) You like jazz, ___? b) She isn't married, ___? c) They went home, ___? d) He can drive, ___? e) You haven't met Tom, ___?

### Answers

**1.** a) Who phoned you? b) Who did you phone? c) What ran into the road? d) What did she see? **2.** a) Could you tell me where the toilet is? b) Do you know if this train stops at York? c) I wonder how much it cost. d) Do you know what time the bank closes? **3.** a) don't you b) is she c) didn't they d) can't he e) have you.

## Unit 20 · Then and now

**Goals:** talk about past habits and how things have changed. **Grammar:** *used to*, *would* for past habits, *be / get used to*.

![](u20.png)

### Text

When I was a child, we lived in a village in Yorkshire. Life used to be very different. We didn't use to have a television, and nobody had a mobile phone. There used to be only one shop, and it closed at five.

Every summer my cousins would come to stay. We would get up early, take some sandwiches and spend the whole day by the river. My grandfather would tell us stories in the evening.

I moved to Manchester for work ten years ago. At first I couldn't sleep because of the traffic, but I soon got used to it. Now I'm used to the noise, the crowds and the pace of life. I still miss the silence, though. Did we use to be happier? I'm not sure. But we certainly used to have more time.

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| childhood | infancia |
| village / town / city | pueblo / ciudad pequeña / ciudad |
| the countryside | el campo |
| crowd / crowded | multitud / abarrotado |
| pace of life | ritmo de vida |
| neighbour | vecino |
| miss | echar de menos |
| change (noun and verb) | cambio; cambiar |
| nowadays | hoy en día |
| in those days | en aquella época |
| no longer / not any more | ya no |
| still | todavía, sigo… |
| at first | al principio |
| grow up | crecer |
| bring up (children) | criar, educar |

### Grammar

**1. *used to* + verb: past habits and states that are not true now**

| Affirmative | Negative | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| We **used to live** in a village. | We **didn't use to have** a TV. | **Did** you **use to be** happier? |

*Used to* exists only in the past. For present habits use the present simple with *usually*: *I **usually** walk to work.*

**2. *would* + verb: repeated actions in the past**

*Every summer my cousins **would come** to stay.*

*Would* is only for actions, not for states: *We **used to live** there* (not «would live»). *I **used to have** long hair* (not «would have»).

**3. *be used to* and *get used to* + noun or *-ing***

| Form | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| used to + verb | past habit | I **used to drive** to work. |
| be used to + *-ing* | it is normal for me now | I**'m used to driving** on the left. |
| get used to + *-ing* | become accustomed | I'm **getting used to driving** on the left. |

**4. *still, any more, no longer***

*I **still** miss the silence. · We do**n't** live there **any more**. · The shop **no longer** exists.*

### Sounds & Spelling

*Used to* is pronounced /ˈjuːstə/: the *s* is /s/ and the *d* disappears. It sounds the same as *use to*.

Compare the verb *use* /juːz/: *I **used** /juːzd/ a knife to open it.*

*Would* is /wʊd/, with a silent *l*, and it is often reduced to *'d*: *We'd get up early.*

### Watch out!

- «Suelo levantarme temprano» = *I **usually** get up early*, not «I use to get up». *Used to* never refers to the present.
- After *did*, write *use to*: *Did you **use** to…?*, *didn't **use** to*.
- *Be used to* takes *-ing*: *I'm used to **getting** up early*, not «used to get up».
- «Ya no fumo» = *I don't smoke **any more*** or *I **no longer** smoke*, not «I already don't smoke».
- *City* is big; *town* is smaller; *village* is very small. «Pueblo» is usually *village* or *town*, not «people».

### Exercises

**1.** Rewrite with *used to*.
a) I had long hair when I was young. b) She didn't like vegetables. c) Did you play an instrument at school?

**2.** Where can you also use *would*? Write *yes* or *no*.
a) We used to go camping every August. b) He used to be very shy. c) My mum used to read to us at night. d) I used to know his address.

**3.** Complete with the correct form of *be used to* or *get used to*.
a) I've lived here for years, so I ___ the rain. b) It took me a month to ___ the food. c) She isn't ___ working at night yet.

**4.** Translate.
a) Ya no vivo allí. b) Suelo cenar tarde. c) Antes fumaba.

### Answers

**1.** a) I used to have long hair. b) She didn't use to like vegetables. c) Did you use to play an instrument at school? **2.** a) yes b) no c) yes d) no. **3.** a) 'm used to b) get used to c) used to. **4.** a) I don't live there any more. b) I usually have dinner late. c) I used to smoke.

## Unit 21 · Telling stories

**Goals:** tell an anecdote in order, make a story interesting. **Grammar:** narrative tenses: past simple, past continuous and past perfect; linkers of time.

![](u21.png)

### Text

Last year my sister and I flew to Rome for a long weekend. We had booked the flights months before, and we had been looking forward to the trip for ages.

When we arrived at the airport, I realised that I had left my passport at home. I couldn't believe it. I had checked my bag three times! My sister was laughing, but I was nearly crying.

By the time I got home in a taxi, the plane had already taken off. Fortunately, the airline put me on the next flight. While I was waiting, I met a retired pilot who had flown all over the world. In the end, I arrived only five hours late, and my sister had already found the best ice cream in the city.

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| realise | darse cuenta |
| look forward to | tener muchas ganas de |
| take off / land | despegar / aterrizar |
| check in | facturar; registrarse |
| boarding pass | tarjeta de embarque |
| delay / delayed | retraso / retrasado |
| queue | cola; hacer cola |
| set off | ponerse en camino |
| in the end | al final |
| at last | por fin |
| eventually | finalmente, al cabo de un tiempo |
| by the time | para cuando |
| fortunately / unfortunately | por suerte / por desgracia |
| to my surprise | para mi sorpresa |
| for ages | durante muchísimo tiempo |

### Grammar

**1. Past perfect: *had* + past participle**

It is «the past of the past»: an action that happened before another past action.

*When we arrived, I realised that I **had left** my passport at home.* (First I left it; later I realised.)

| Affirmative | Negative | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I **had** (I**'d**) checked | I **hadn't** checked | **Had** you checked? |

**2. Three narrative tenses**

| Tense | Use in a story | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Past simple | the main events, in order | I got home and took a taxi. |
| Past continuous | the background; actions in progress | My sister was laughing. |
| Past perfect | what happened earlier | The plane had already taken off. |

Compare: *When I arrived, the plane **took off**.* (I saw it.) / *When I arrived, the plane **had taken off**.* (I missed it.)

**3. Past perfect continuous: *had been* + *-ing***

For a longer activity before a moment in the past: *We **had been looking** forward to the trip for ages.*

**4. Linkers of time**

*before · after · when · while · as soon as · by the time · until · then · afterwards · in the end · eventually*

### Sounds & Spelling

In a story, *had* almost disappears: *I'd left* /aɪd ˈleft/, *she'd found* /ʃiːd ˈfaʊnd/, *the plane had* /ðə ˈpleɪn əd/.

Listen for the difference: *I left* / *I'd left* · *they arrived* / *they'd arrived*. Often only the context tells you.

### Watch out!

- *Eventually* means «finalmente», not «eventualmente» (= *possibly*). *Actually* means «en realidad», not «actualmente» (= *currently, nowadays*).
- ***At the end** of the film* (the final part) / ***In the end**, we stayed* (the final result).
- *Realise* = «darse cuenta». «Realizar» = *carry out, do*.
- *Look forward to* takes *-ing*: *I'm looking forward to **seeing** you*, not «to see you».
- Do not use the past perfect for every past action; use it only when you need to show that one thing happened before another.

### Exercises

**1.** Past simple or past perfect?
a) When I got to the station, the train ___ (leave). b) She ___ (not eat) anything because she ___ (have) lunch. c) I ___ (recognise) him at once: we ___ (meet) before. d) After they ___ (finish), they ___ (go) home.

**2.** Choose the correct form.
a) While I *waited / was waiting*, it *started / had started* to snow. b) I was tired because I *worked / had been working* all day. c) By the time we arrived, the film *already began / had already begun*.

**3.** Complete with *at the end, in the end, eventually* or *by the time*.
a) ___ of the lesson, we did a test. b) We waited an hour, and ___ a taxi came. c) ___ I woke up, everybody had left. d) I wanted the red one, but ___ I bought the blue one.

### Answers

**1.** a) had left b) didn't eat, had had c) recognised, had met d) had finished, went. **2.** a) was waiting, started b) had been working c) had already begun. **3.** a) At the end b) eventually c) By the time d) in the end.

## Unit 22 · How long have you been…?

**Goals:** talk about activities that started in the past and continue, explain present results. **Grammar:** present perfect continuous and present perfect simple.

![](u22.png)

### Dialogue

**Tom:** You look exhausted. What have you been doing?
**Lucía:** I've been revising for my exam all morning. I've read four chapters.
**Tom:** How long have you been studying English now?
**Lucía:** I've been learning it for three years, but I've only been living here since January.
**Tom:** Your English has improved a lot.
**Lucía:** Thanks. I've been watching British series without subtitles.
**Tom:** That explains it. Have you finished the book I lent you?
**Lucía:** Not yet. I've been reading it on the bus. I've got to chapter ten.
**Tom:** Why are your hands blue?
**Lucía:** Oh! I've been painting my room.

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| revise (for an exam) | repasar |
| take / sit an exam | hacer un examen |
| pass / fail | aprobar / suspender |
| mark | nota |
| improve | mejorar |
| subtitles | subtítulos |
| chapter | capítulo |
| lend / borrow | prestar / tomar prestado |
| exhausted | agotado |
| lately / recently | últimamente / recientemente |
| all day / all morning | todo el día / toda la mañana |
| so far | hasta ahora |
| attend (a course) | asistir a |
| skill | destreza, habilidad |
| progress | progreso |

### Grammar

**1. Present perfect continuous: *have / has been* + *-ing***

*I**'ve been revising** all morning. · She **hasn't been sleeping** well. · How long **have** you **been learning** English?*

**2. Continuous or simple?**

| Present perfect continuous | Present perfect simple |
| --- | --- |
| focus on the **activity** and its duration | focus on the **result** or a number |
| *I've been reading your book.* (not finished) | *I've read your book.* (finished) |
| *She's been writing emails all day.* | *She's written twenty emails.* |
| *How long…?* | *How much…? How many…?* |

**3. Visible results now**

*Your hands are blue.* — *I**'ve been painting**.* · *The ground is wet. It**'s been raining**.*

**4. State verbs take the simple form**

*I**'ve known** him for years* (not «I've been knowing»). The same is true of *be, have* (possession), *like, believe, understand*.

With *live* and *work* both forms are possible with little difference: *I've lived / I've been living here since January.*

### Sounds & Spelling

In this tense only the main verb is stressed. *Have* and *been* are weak: *I've been waiting* /aɪv bɪn ˈweɪtɪŋ/. *How long have you been learning?* /haʊ ˈlɒŋ əv ju bɪn ˈlɜːnɪŋ/.

English is stress-timed: the stressed syllables come at regular intervals, and the small words between them are squeezed.

### Watch out!

- «Llevo tres años estudiando inglés» = *I**'ve been studying** English **for** three years*, not «I am studying English since three years» or «I carry three years…».
- «¿Cuánto tiempo llevas aquí?» = *How long **have you been** here?*, not «How long are you here?».
- *Lend* = dar prestado; *borrow* = tomar prestado: *Can you **lend** me a pen? / Can I **borrow** a pen?*
- «Aprobar un examen» = ***pass** an exam*. «Hacer un examen» = ***take** an exam*. *Approve* is «dar el visto bueno».
- *Progress* is uncountable: *I've made a lot of **progress***, not «many progresses».

### Exercises

**1.** Complete with the present perfect continuous.
a) It ___ (rain) all day. b) How long ___ you ___ (wait)? c) I ___ (not feel) well lately.

**2.** Simple or continuous?
a) I ___ (write) three emails this morning. b) She's tired because she ___ (run). c) We ___ (know) each other since school. d) He ___ (paint) the kitchen, and it isn't finished yet. e) How many pages ___ you ___ (read)?

**3.** Translate.
a) Llevo dos horas esperando. b) ¿Cuánto tiempo llevas viviendo aquí? c) ¿Me prestas tu libro?

### Answers

**1.** a) 's been raining b) have … been waiting c) haven't been feeling. **2.** a) 've written b) 's been running c) 've known d) 's been painting e) have … read. **3.** a) I've been waiting for two hours. b) How long have you been living here? c) Can you lend me your book? / Can I borrow your book?

## Unit 23 · Looking ahead

**Goals:** make predictions, talk about probability and about the future from different points of view. **Grammar:** future forms compared, *may / might*, future continuous, *will probably / definitely*.

![](u23.png)

### Text

What will life be like in 2050? Nobody knows for certain, but scientists have made some predictions.

Most people will probably live in cities, and many of us will be working from home. Cars will definitely be electric, and they may drive themselves. We might not need to learn to drive at all.

The climate is going to get warmer: we can already see the evidence. Some experts think that Britain could have the weather of southern France. Summers are likely to be hotter and drier.

Will robots take our jobs? They might do the boring ones. But this time tomorrow I'll be sitting at my desk as usual, and I doubt a robot will be writing this for me. I'm meeting my editor at ten, and she certainly won't accept that excuse.

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| prediction | predicción |
| evidence | pruebas, indicios |
| expert / scientist | experto / científico |
| likely / unlikely | probable / improbable |
| probably / definitely | probablemente / sin duda |
| I doubt (that)… | dudo que… |
| I expect (that)… | supongo que… |
| for certain | con seguridad |
| work from home | trabajar desde casa |
| artificial intelligence | inteligencia artificial |
| device | dispositivo |
| develop | desarrollar |
| increase / decrease | aumentar / disminuir |
| run out of | quedarse sin |
| in the long run | a la larga |

### Grammar

**1. Future forms compared**

| Form | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| will | prediction from opinion; sudden decision; promise | I think cars will be electric. |
| going to | prediction from present evidence; plan | Look at those clouds. It's going to rain. |
| present continuous | fixed arrangement | I'm meeting my editor at ten. |
| present simple | timetables | The train leaves at 9.15. |

**2. Degrees of certainty**

| Certainty | Form |
| --- | --- |
| 100% | will **definitely** / **certainly** |
| 75% | will **probably** · is **likely to** |
| 50% | **may** · **might** · **could** |
| 25% | **probably won't** · is **unlikely to** |
| 0% | **definitely won't** |

Word order: *will **probably** be* but ***probably** won't be*.

**3. Future continuous: *will be* + *-ing***

An action in progress at a moment in the future: *This time tomorrow I**'ll be sitting** at my desk. · Many of us **will be working** from home.*

**4. *may* and *might***

*They **may** drive themselves. · We **might not** need to learn.* There is no *to* and no *-s*: *she might come*.

### Sounds & Spelling

Many nouns ending in *-tion* and *-sion* are almost the same as in Spanish, but the stress is always on the syllable before the ending, and the ending is weak:

*pre**DIC**tion* /prɪˈdɪkʃn/ · *infor**MA**tion* /ˌɪnfəˈmeɪʃn/ · *de**CI**sion* /dɪˈsɪʒn/ · *tele**VI**sion* /ˈtelɪvɪʒn/

*-tion* is /ʃn/ and *-sion* after a vowel is /ʒn/.

### Watch out!

- Spanish uses the subjunctive after «quizá» and «puede que». English uses *may* or *might*: «Puede que llueva» = *It **might** rain*, not «It's possible that it rains».
- *Probably* goes after *will* but before *won't*: *She'll **probably** come. / She **probably** won't come.*
- «Dudo que venga» = *I doubt he**'ll come***.
- *Evidence* is uncountable: *a lot of evidence*, not «evidences».
- «El futuro» with a timetable is the present simple: *The film **starts** at eight.*

### Exercises

**1.** Choose the best form.
a) Look out! That glass *will / is going to* fall. b) I think Spain *will / is going to* win, but who knows? c) I can't come on Friday: I *will see / 'm seeing* the dentist. d) The plane *leaves / will leave* at 6.40.

**2.** Put the adverb in the correct place.
a) They will be late. (probably) b) She won't call. (definitely) c) It will rain. (certainly) d) We won't have time. (probably)

**3.** Complete with the future continuous.
a) At nine tomorrow I ___ (work). b) This time next week we ___ (lie) on the beach. c) Don't phone at eight: they ___ (have) dinner.

**4.** Rewrite with *might*.
a) Perhaps she'll come. b) Maybe they won't like it.

### Answers

**1.** a) is going to b) will c) 'm seeing d) leaves. **2.** a) They will probably be late. b) She definitely won't call. c) It will certainly rain. d) We probably won't have time. **3.** a) 'll be working b) 'll be lying c) 'll be having. **4.** a) She might come. b) They might not like it.

## Unit 24 · Imagine…

**Goals:** talk about imaginary situations, give advice, express wishes about the present. **Grammar:** second conditional, *wish* + past, *if I were you*.

![](u24.png)

### Dialogue

**Jack:** What would you do if you won the lottery?
**Emma:** If I won a million pounds, I'd buy a house by the sea. What about you?
**Jack:** I wouldn't stop working. I'd be bored if I didn't have a job.
**Emma:** Really? If I were you, I'd travel round the world.
**Jack:** I wish I had more free time. And I wish I could speak another language.
**Emma:** You could learn Spanish if you really wanted to.
**Jack:** I would if I had the time. If only the classes weren't so late!
**Emma:** If I were you, I'd stop making excuses.

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| win the lottery | ganar la lotería |
| earn money | ganar dinero (trabajando) |
| spend / save | gastar / ahorrar |
| waste | malgastar |
| afford | permitirse |
| be worth | valer |
| owe | deber (dinero) |
| debt / loan | deuda / préstamo |
| salary / wage | sueldo / jornal |
| tax | impuesto |
| rich / wealthy / well-off | rico / adinerado / acomodado |
| broke (informal) | sin blanca |
| make excuses | poner excusas |
| If only… | Ojalá… |
| I wish… | Ojalá…, me gustaría que… |

### Grammar

**1. Second conditional: imaginary or unlikely situations**

*if* + past simple, *would* + verb

| Condition | Result |
| --- | --- |
| If I **won** a million pounds, | I**'d buy** a house. |
| If I **didn't have** a job, | I**'d be** bored. |
| What **would** you **do** | if you **won**? |

The past form here does not refer to past time. It shows that the situation is not real.

**2. First or second conditional?**

*If I **have** time, I**'ll help** you.* (It is possible.) / *If I **had** time, I**'d help** you.* (But I haven't.)

**3. *If I were you…***

For advice we say *were* for all persons: *If I **were** you, I**'d travel**. · If she **were** here, she'd know what to do.* (*Was* is common in informal speech.)

**4. *wish* + past: wishes about the present**

*I wish I **had** more time.* (I haven't.) · *I wish I **could** speak French.* (I can't.) · *I wish it **weren't** so late.* · ***If only** I **knew**!*

*could* and *might* can replace *would* in the result: *You **could** learn Spanish if you wanted to.*

### Sounds & Spelling

*Would* contracts to *'d*, and so does *had*: *I'd buy* (= I would buy) / *I'd bought* (= I had bought). The verb form after it tells you which one it is.

*Wouldn't* /ˈwʊdnt/, *couldn't* /ˈkʊdnt/ and *shouldn't* /ˈʃʊdnt/ have a silent *l* and no vowel between *d* and *n*.

### Watch out!

- No *would* in the *if* part: *If I **had** money*, not «If I would have money». Spanish uses the subjunctive («si tuviera») and English uses the past.
- «Ojalá tuviera más tiempo» = *I wish I **had** more time*, not «I wish I have» or «I wish I would have».
- ***Win*** a prize, a match, the lottery. ***Earn*** money for your work. ***Beat*** another team: *We beat them*, not «We won them».
- «No puedo permitírmelo» = *I can't **afford** it*.
- *Spend* money **on** something: *I spend a lot **on** books*, not «in books».

### Answers

**1.** a) had, 'd drive b) would be, lived c) would … do, saw d) weren't, 'd come. **2.** a) rains b) were c) heat. **3.** a) I wish I had a garden. b) I wish I could swim. c) I wish it weren't raining. d) I wish I didn't live far away.

## Unit 25 · Regrets

**Goals:** talk about how the past could have been different, express regret and criticism. **Grammar:** third conditional, *wish* + past perfect, *should have*.

![](u25.png)

### Text

I often think about the summer of 2012. I had a place at university in Edinburgh, but I decided not to go. A friend offered me a job in his restaurant, and I took it.

If I had gone to university, I would have studied architecture. I wouldn't have met my wife, though, because she was a customer at the restaurant. If she hadn't ordered the fish that night, we would never have spoken.

Do I have regrets? A few. I should have listened to my parents about saving money. I shouldn't have bought that motorbike. And I wish I had learnt to play the piano when I was young.

But if I had done everything differently, I wouldn't be here now. On the whole, I think I made the right choice.

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| regret | arrepentirse de; arrepentimiento |
| choice / choose | elección / elegir |
| decision / decide | decisión / decidir |
| make a decision | tomar una decisión |
| opportunity / chance | oportunidad |
| miss an opportunity | perder una oportunidad |
| turn down (an offer) | rechazar |
| take a risk | arriesgarse |
| blame | culpar |
| fault | culpa |
| apologise (for) | disculparse (por) |
| on the whole | en general |
| differently | de otra manera |
| instead (of) | en lugar (de) |
| It's a pity / a shame | es una pena |

### Grammar

**1. Third conditional: an imaginary past**

*if* + past perfect, *would have* + past participle

| Condition (it didn't happen) | Result (it didn't happen) |
| --- | --- |
| If I **had gone** to university, | I **would have studied** architecture. |
| If she **hadn't ordered** the fish, | we **would never have spoken**. |

**2. *should have* / *shouldn't have* + participle: criticism and regret**

*I **should have listened** to my parents.* (I didn't.) · *I **shouldn't have bought** that motorbike.* (I did.)

**3. *wish* + past perfect: regrets about the past**

*I wish I **had learnt** the piano. · I wish I **hadn't said** that. · **If only** we **had left** earlier!*

**4. Mixed conditional: past condition, present result**

*If I **had done** everything differently, I **wouldn't be** here now.*

| | Condition | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Second | If I had time (now), | I would help. |
| Third | If I had had time (then), | I would have helped. |
| Mixed | If I had studied (then), | I would have a better job (now). |

### Sounds & Spelling

*Have* is very weak after a modal. It sounds like /əv/, or just /ə/:

*would have* /ˈwʊdəv/ · *should have* /ˈʃʊdəv/ · *could have* /ˈkʊdəv/ · *wouldn't have* /ˈwʊdntəv/

This is why some native speakers wrongly write «would of». Always write *have*.

### Watch out!

- No *would* in the *if* part: *If I **had known***, not «If I would have known». Spanish «si lo hubiera sabido» = *if I had known*.
- «Debería haber ido» = *I **should have gone***. «Podría haber ido» = *I **could have gone***.
- «Tomar una decisión» = ***make** a decision*, not «take a decision» in everyday British English.
- *Regret* + *-ing*: *I regret **buying** it*.
- «Es culpa mía» = *It's my **fault***. *Blame* is the verb: *Don't blame me*.

**1.** Complete with the third conditional.
a) If I ___ (know), I ___ (tell) you. b) We ___ (not miss) the train if we ___ (leave) earlier. c) If she ___ (study), she ___ (pass). d) What ___ you ___ (do) if you ___ (lose) your job?

**2.** Write a sentence with *should have* or *shouldn't have*.
a) I ate too much. b) He didn't take an umbrella. c) You were rude to her. d) We didn't book a table.

a) I didn't go to the party. b) I spent all my money. c) I didn't learn to drive.

### Answers

**1.** a) had known, would have told b) wouldn't have missed, had left c) had studied, would have passed d) would … have done, had lost. **2.** a) I shouldn't have eaten so much. b) He should have taken an umbrella. c) You shouldn't have been rude to her. d) We should have booked a table. **3.** a) I wish I had gone to the party. b) I wish I hadn't spent all my money. c) I wish I had learnt to drive.

## Unit 26 · It must be…

**Goals:** make deductions, speculate, talk about ability, permission and obligation. **Grammar:** modals of deduction (*must, might, can't*), *be able to*, *be allowed to*, review of modals.

![](u26.png)

### Dialogue

**Emma:** Whose bag is this? Someone's left it on the bench.
**Jack:** It must be a student's. Look, there are books inside.
**Emma:** It can't be a student's. These are law books, and there's no law school here.
**Jack:** It might belong to a lawyer, then. Is there a name?
**Emma:** There's a train ticket to Cardiff. The owner could be Welsh.
**Jack:** Or they may just be visiting. Are we allowed to open the wallet?
**Emma:** I don't think we should. Oh, there's a phone. It's ringing!
**Jack:** That must be the owner. Answer it!
**Emma:** Hello? … Yes, we've found it. … She'll be able to collect it in ten minutes.

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| belong to | pertenecer a |
| owner | dueño |
| wallet / purse | cartera / monedero |
| bench | banco (para sentarse) |
| clue | pista |
| guess | adivinar; suposición |
| suppose | suponer |
| obviously | evidentemente |
| it seems / it looks | parece |
| look like | parecerse a |
| be allowed to | tener permiso para |
| manage to | lograr |
| succeed in | conseguir |
| law / lawyer | derecho; ley / abogado |
| Whose…? | ¿De quién…? |

### Grammar

**1. Deduction about the present**

| Modal | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **must** | I'm sure it is true | It **must** be a student's. |
| **may / might / could** | it is possible | It **might** belong to a lawyer. |
| **can't** | I'm sure it is not true | It **can't** be a student's. |

With an action in progress: *They **may be visiting**. · He **must be joking**.*

**2. Ability: *can, could, be able to***

*Can* has only two forms (*can, could*). For other tenses use *be able to*: *She**'ll be able to** collect it. · I**'ve** never **been able to** dance.*

For one successful action in the past use *was able to* or *managed to*, not *could*: *The door was locked, but I **managed to** open it.*

**3. Permission: *can, may, be allowed to***

*Are we **allowed to** open it? · You **can't** park here. · **May** I come in?* (formal)

**4. Review of modals**

| Function | Modals |
| --- | --- |
| Obligation | must, have to |
| Prohibition | mustn't, can't |
| No obligation | don't have to, needn't |
| Advice | should, ought to |
| Possibility | may, might, could |

### Sounds & Spelling

*Can* is weak in sentences: *I can swim* /aɪ kən ˈswɪm/. *Can't* is long and strong in British English: /kɑːnt/, the same vowel as *car*. (American English says /kænt/.)

So in British English the vowel tells you the difference: *I can /kən/ come* — *I can't /kɑːnt/ come*.

The *t* in *must* often disappears before a consonant: *must be* /ˈmʌsbi/.

### Watch out!

- The opposite of *must* for deduction is *can't*, not *mustn't*: *It **can't** be true*. «No puede ser verdad».
- Spanish uses «deber de» and the future for deduction («Será de un estudiante»). English uses *must*: *It **must** be a student's*, not «It will be».
- *Whose* (¿de quién?) and *who's* (= who is) sound the same.
- *Lawyer* is «abogado». «Avocado» is «aguacate».
- A *bench* is for sitting on; a *bank* is for money. A *wallet* is for notes; a *purse* (BrE) is for coins.

### Exercises

**1.** Complete with *must, might* or *can't*.
a) She's been working all day. She ___ be tired. b) He ___ be at home: his car isn't there. c) I'm not sure, but it ___ rain later. d) You've just eaten! You ___ be hungry.

**2.** Complete with the correct form of *be able to*.
a) I'd love to ___ sing. b) She hasn't ___ sleep. c) We won't ___ come tomorrow.

**3.** Rewrite with the word in brackets.
a) Perhaps he's ill. (might) b) I'm sure this is her coat. (must) c) I'm sure that isn't Tom. (can't) d) We have permission to park here. (allowed)

### Answers

**1.** a) must b) can't c) might d) can't. **2.** a) be able to b) been able to c) be able to. **3.** a) He might be ill. b) This must be her coat. c) That can't be Tom. d) We are allowed to park here.

## Unit 27 · She said that…

**Goals:** report what people said, asked and told you to do; pass on messages. **Grammar:** reported speech, *say* and *tell*, reported questions and commands.

![](u27.png)

### Text

I had a strange conversation with my landlord yesterday. He rang at eight in the morning and asked me if I was at home. I told him that I was having breakfast.

He said that he needed to check the boiler and asked whether he could come that afternoon. I explained that I would be at work until six. He told me not to worry and said he had a key.

When I got home, there was a note on the table. It said that the boiler had been repaired, but that he had accidentally broken a window. He promised to fix it the following day and asked me to keep the heating on. He also wanted to know why I had so many plants.

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| landlord / landlady | casero / casera |
| tenant | inquilino |
| rent | alquiler; alquilar |
| deposit | fianza |
| bill | factura |
| boiler | caldera |
| heating | calefacción |
| repair / fix | reparar / arreglar |
| note / message | nota / mensaje |
| explain | explicar |
| promise | prometer |
| warn | advertir |
| remind | recordar (a alguien) |
| suggest | sugerir |
| complain | quejarse |

### Grammar

**1. Tense changes**

When the reporting verb is in the past, the tense usually moves one step back.

| Direct speech | Reported speech |
| --- | --- |
| "I **need** to check the boiler." | He said he **needed** to check the boiler. |
| "I **am having** breakfast." | I told him I **was having** breakfast. |
| "I **broke** a window." / "I **have broken**…" | He said he **had broken** a window. |
| "I **will** fix it." | He promised he **would** fix it. |
| "I **can** come." | He said he **could** come. |

**2. Other changes**

*I → he / she · my → his / her · this → that · here → there · today → that day · tomorrow → the next / following day · yesterday → the day before · now → then*

**3. *say* and *tell***

*He **said** (that) he had a key.* / *He **told me** (that) he had a key.* *Tell* needs a person.

**4. Reported questions**

Statement word order; no *do*; no question mark.

| Direct | Reported |
| --- | --- |
| "**Are you** at home?" | He asked me **if / whether I was** at home. |
| "Why **do you have** so many plants?" | He asked why **I had** so many plants. |

**5. Reported commands and requests: verb + person + (*not*) *to* + verb**

*"Don't worry." → He **told me not to worry**. · "Please keep the heating on." → He **asked me to keep** the heating on.*

### Sounds & Spelling

*Said* is /sed/: it rhymes with *bed*, not with *paid*. *Says* is /sez/.

The word *that* is weak in reported speech, /ðət/, and it is often left out: *He said (that) he was tired.*

### Watch out!

- *Say something* / *tell someone something*: *He **told me** the truth* / *He **said** nothing*, not «He said me» or «He told that…».
- *Explain* and *suggest* need *to*: *He **explained** the problem **to me***, not «explained me the problem».
- «Me recordó que llamara» = *He **reminded** me to call*. *Remember* is what you do yourself.
- Reported questions keep statement order: *She asked where **I lived***, not «where did I live».
- *Rent* is what you pay for a flat. «Renta» (income) is *income*. A *bill* is a «factura»; a *note* is a «billete» or a «nota».

**1.** Complete with *said* or *told*.
a) She ___ she was tired. b) He ___ me he was leaving. c) They ___ us to wait. d) I ___ that I agreed.

**2.** Report the sentences.
a) "I'm hungry," said Tom. b) "I'll call you tomorrow," she told me. c) "We've finished," they said. d) "I can't find my keys," he said.

**3.** Report the questions and commands.
a) "Where do you work?" she asked me. b) "Have you seen Emma?" he asked. c) "Sit down," the teacher told us. d) "Please don't be late," she asked him.

### Answers

**1.** a) said b) told c) told d) said. **2.** a) Tom said he was hungry. b) She told me she would call me the next day. c) They said they had finished. d) He said he couldn't find his keys. **3.** a) She asked me where I worked. b) He asked if I had seen Emma. c) The teacher told us to sit down. d) She asked him not to be late.

## Unit 28 · People, places and things

**Goals:** define and describe people, places and things; add information; write a description. **Grammar:** defining and non-defining relative clauses, articles revisited.

![](u28.png)

### Text

Bath, which is in the south-west of England, is one of the most beautiful cities in the country. It is a place where history is everywhere you look.

The Romans, who arrived in the first century, built the baths that give the city its name. The water that fills them still comes out of the ground at 46 degrees.

Jane Austen, whose novels are read all over the world, lived here for five years. The house where she stayed is now a museum. The woman who showed us round was an expert who knew every detail of the writer's life.

The thing I liked most was the Royal Crescent, a curved street of thirty houses which was built in the 1770s. It is the kind of place that you never forget.

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| century | siglo |
| building | edificio |
| bridge | puente |
| castle | castillo |
| tower | torre |
| square | plaza |
| ancient | antiguo (muy antiguo) |
| modern | moderno |
| historic | histórico |
| lively | animado |
| quiet / peaceful | tranquilo |
| picturesque | pintoresco |
| be located in | estar situado en |
| be famous for | ser famoso por |
| show someone round | enseñar un sitio a alguien |

### Grammar

**1. Defining relative clauses**

They say which person or thing we mean. There are no commas.

| For | Pronoun | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| people | who / that | The woman **who** showed us round was an expert. |
| things | which / that | The baths **that** give the city its name… |
| places | where | The house **where** she stayed… |
| possession | whose | A writer **whose** novels are famous… |
| time | when | The year **when** I was born… |

When the pronoun is the **object** of the clause, you can leave it out: *The thing (**that**) I liked most… · The man (**who**) I met…*

**2. Non-defining relative clauses**

They add extra information. They always have commas, and you cannot use *that* or leave out the pronoun.

*Bath, **which** is in the south-west, is beautiful. · Jane Austen, **whose** novels are read everywhere, lived here.*

**3. Prepositions at the end**

*The hotel (that) we stayed **at**. · The people (who) I work **with**.* (Formal: *the hotel **at which** we stayed*.)

**4. Articles revisited**

| Use | Article | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| first mention; one of many; jobs | a / an | She's **an** expert. |
| known or unique; superlatives | the | **the** Romans, **the** most beautiful |
| things in general (plural, uncountable) | — | **History** is everywhere. I like **cities**. |
| most countries, cities, streets | — | **England**, **Bath** |
| rivers, seas, plural countries | the | **the** Thames, **the** United Kingdom |

### Sounds & Spelling

*The* has two pronunciations: /ðə/ before a consonant sound (*the city, the house, the university*) and /ðiː/ before a vowel sound (*the end, the hour, the expert*).

In writing, the commas of a non-defining clause are pauses in speech: *Bath, | which is in the south-west, | is beautiful.*

### Watch out!

- Do not repeat the subject or object: *The woman who showed us round*, not «The woman who **she** showed us round». *The book that I read*, not «that I read **it**».
- «Lo que» = *what*: ***What** I liked most was the Crescent*, not «That what I liked».
- No article for things in general: ***Life** is short*, ***People** are strange*, *I love **music***, not «the life», «the people», «the music».
- *Historic* = important in history; *historical* = related to history (*a historical novel*).
- «Antiguo» is *old* or *ancient*. *Antique* is for valuable old objects.

# Level A1 · First steps

At the end of this level you can introduce yourself, ask simple questions, order in a café and talk about your day and your weekend.

## Unit 1 · Hello! Nice to meet you

**Goals:** say hello, introduce yourself, spell your name. **Grammar:** the verb *be* (singular), subject pronouns.

![](u01.png)

### Dialogue

**Tom:** Hello! I'm Tom. What's your name?
**Lucía:** Hi, Tom. My name's Lucía.
**Tom:** Nice to meet you, Lucía. How do you spell that?
**Lucía:** L-U-C-I-A. Nice to meet you too.
**Tom:** Where are you from?
**Lucía:** I'm from Spain. I'm Spanish. And you?
**Tom:** I'm from England. I'm a teacher. Welcome to London!
**Lucía:** Thank you. See you tomorrow!
**Tom:** Bye!

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| hello / hi | hola |
| goodbye / bye | adiós |
| good morning | buenos días |
| good afternoon | buenas tardes |
| good evening | buenas tardes / noches (al llegar) |
| good night | buenas noches (al irse a dormir) |
| please / thank you | por favor / gracias |
| yes / no | sí / no |
| sorry | perdón, lo siento |
| name | nombre |
| teacher / student | profesor / estudiante |
| nice to meet you | encantado, encantada |
| see you tomorrow | hasta mañana |

Numbers 0–10: zero, one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten.

Countries and nationalities: Spain → Spanish · England → English · Ireland → Irish · France → French · Germany → German · Italy → Italian.

### Grammar

**1. The verb *be* (singular)**

| Full form | Short form | Negative | Question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I am | I'm | I'm not | Am I…? |
| you are | you're | you aren't | Are you…? |
| he is | he's | he isn't | Is he…? |
| she is | she's | she isn't | Is she…? |
| it is | it's | it isn't | Is it…? |

Short answers: *Are you Spanish?* — *Yes, I am.* / *No, I'm not.*

**2. Subject pronouns**

English has one word for *tú* and *usted*: **you**. *He* is for a man, *she* for a woman, *it* for a thing or an animal.

**3. Questions with *what*, *where*, *how***

*What's your name? · Where are you from? · How are you?* — *Fine, thanks.*

### Sounds & Spelling

The alphabet. Be careful with the names of the vowels:

| Letter | Name | Letter | Name |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | /eɪ/ | I | /aɪ/ |
| E | /iː/ | O | /əʊ/ |
| U | /juː/ | Y | /waɪ/ |

Other difficult letters: G /dʒiː/, J /dʒeɪ/, H /eɪtʃ/, R /ɑː/, W /ˈdʌbəljuː/, Z /zed/ (American English: /ziː/).

### Watch out!

- English always needs a subject. «Es tarde» = ***It's** late*, not «Is late». «Soy Lucía» = ***I'm** Lucía*.
- The letter **E** is called /iː/ and the letter **I** is called /aɪ/. They are the opposite of what a Spanish speaker expects.
- Nationalities have a capital letter: *I'm **S**panish*.
- *I* is always a capital letter.

### Exercises

**1.** Complete with *am*, *is* or *are*.
a) I ___ Tom. b) She ___ a student. c) ___ you from Spain? d) It ___ late. e) He ___ not English.

**2.** Write the short form.
a) I am → ___ b) you are → ___ c) she is not → ___ d) what is → ___

**3.** Put the words in order.
a) name / your / what's / ? b) from / are / where / you / ? c) to / nice / you / meet

### Answers

**1.** a) am b) is c) Are d) is e) is. **2.** a) I'm b) you're c) she isn't d) what's. **3.** a) What's your name? b) Where are you from? c) Nice to meet you.

## Unit 2 · This is my family

**Goals:** introduce other people, talk about your family and say your age. **Grammar:** *be* (plural), possessive adjectives, *'s*, *a / an*, plurals.

![](u02.png)

### Text

This is my family. My mother's name is Helen and my father's name is David. They are from Manchester. I've got a brother and a sister. My brother's name is Jack. He's twenty-five and he's a nurse. My sister Emma is eighteen. She's a student.

My grandparents are old but very happy. Their house is small. We are a big family: I've got six cousins! And this is Max. He isn't a person. He's our dog.

| children | hijos |

Numbers 11–100: eleven, twelve, thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, sixteen, seventeen, eighteen, nineteen, twenty, twenty-one… thirty, forty, fifty, sixty, seventy, eighty, ninety, a hundred.

### Grammar

**1. The verb *be* (plural)**

| Affirmative | Negative | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| we are / we're | we aren't | Are we…? |
| you are / you're | you aren't | Are you…? |
| they are / they're | they aren't | Are they…? |

**2. Possessive adjectives**

| I | you | he | she | it | we | they |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| my | your | his | her | its | our | their |

**3. Possessive *'s***

*My mother**'s** name* = the name of my mother. *Jack**'s** dog* = the dog of Jack.

**4. *a / an* and plurals**

*a* + consonant sound: *a brother, a student*. *an* + vowel sound: *an aunt, an uncle*. Jobs need *a / an*: *He's **a** nurse.*

| Singular | Plural | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| brother, cousin | brothers, cousins | + s |
| bus, watch | buses, watches | + es after s, ch, sh, x |
| family | families | consonant + y → ies |
| child, man, woman, person | children, men, women, people | irregular |

### Sounds & Spelling

Short /ɪ/ and long /iː/: *th**i**s* /ðɪs/ — *th**e**se* /ðiːz/, *s**i**t* — *s**ea**t*, *sh**i**p* — *sh**ee**p*. For /iː/ smile and make the sound longer.

The plural *-s* has three sounds: /s/ *cats, parents* · /z/ *brothers, dogs* · /ɪz/ *buses, watches*.

### Watch out!

- Age uses *be*, not *have*: ***I'm** twenty*, not «I have twenty years».
- *His* and *her* agree with the **owner**, not with the thing: *Anna and **her** brother*; *Tom and **his** sister*.
- ***Parents*** = padres (father and mother). «Parientes» = *relatives*.
- Adjectives have no plural: *two **big** houses*, not «bigs houses».
- Jobs need the article: *She's **a** teacher.*

**2.** Write the plural.
a) sister b) family c) child d) bus e) woman f) cousin

**3.** Correct the mistakes.
a) I have 30 years. b) She is teacher. c) They are olds. d) This is the house of Jack.

### Answers

**1.** a) my b) her c) Their d) our e) his. **2.** a) sisters b) families c) children d) buses e) women f) cousins. **3.** a) I'm 30. b) She is a teacher. c) They are old. d) This is Jack's house.

## Unit 3 · My day

**Goals:** talk about your routine, say the time and the days of the week. **Grammar:** present simple (*I, you, we, they*), adverbs of frequency.

![](u03.png)

### Text

I'm a bus driver in Bristol. I get up at half past five every day. I have a shower, I get dressed and I have breakfast: tea and toast. I start work at seven o'clock.

I have lunch at one. I usually have a sandwich. I finish work at three and I go home. In the evening I cook dinner and I watch TV with my family. We never go to bed late. On Saturdays and Sundays I don't work. I sometimes play football with my friends, and we always have a big Sunday lunch.

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| get up | levantarse |
| have a shower | ducharse |
| get dressed | vestirse |
| have breakfast / lunch / dinner | desayunar / comer / cenar |
| start / finish work | empezar / terminar de trabajar |
| go home | irse a casa |
| go to bed | irse a la cama |
| cook | cocinar |
| watch TV | ver la tele |
| in the morning / afternoon / evening | por la mañana / tarde / tarde-noche |
| at night | por la noche |
| every day | todos los días |
| always / usually / often | siempre / normalmente / a menudo |
| sometimes / never | a veces / nunca |
| early / late | temprano / tarde |

The days of the week: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday.

### Grammar

**1. Present simple: *I, you, we, they***

| Affirmative | Negative | Question | Short answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I work | I don't work | Do I work? | Yes, I do. / No, I don't. |
| you work | you don't work | Do you work? | Yes, you do. / No, you don't. |
| we work | we don't work | Do we work? | Yes, we do. / No, we don't. |
| they work | they don't work | Do they work? | Yes, they do. / No, they don't. |

We use the present simple for habits and routines.

**2. Adverbs of frequency**

They go **before** the main verb, but **after** *be*: *I **always** get up early. · I'm **never** late.*

**3. The time**

| Time | We say |
| --- | --- |
| 7:00 | It's seven o'clock. |
| 7:15 | It's quarter past seven. |
| 7:30 | It's half past seven. |
| 7:45 | It's quarter to eight. |
| 7:10 / 7:50 | It's ten past seven. / It's ten to eight. |

*What time is it?* · *What time do you get up?* — ***At** half past six.* · ***On** Monday.* · ***In** the morning.*

### Sounds & Spelling

The schwa /ə/ is a weak, short vowel. It is never stressed. Listen for it in small words: *I go **to** bed* /tə/, *quarter* /ˈkwɔːtə/, *seven **o'**clock* /əˈklɒk/, *breakf**a**st* /ˈbrekfəst/.

Word stress: in English one syllable is strong: **MON**day, **BREAK**fast, to**DAY**, a**FTER**noon → after**NOON**.

### Watch out!

- The adverb goes before the verb: *I **always** get up at seven*, not «I get up always».
- English days have a capital letter and *on*: ***on M**onday*.
- «Tengo hambre por la mañana» is fine, but meals use *have*: ***have** breakfast*, not «take breakfast» or «eat the breakfast».
- *I get up* has no reflexive pronoun: «me levanto» = *I get up*, not «I get up me».
- *In the evening* = from about 6 p.m. *At night* = when it is dark and you sleep.

### Exercises

**1.** Make the sentences negative.
a) I work on Sundays. b) We have lunch at twelve. c) They watch TV in the morning.

**2.** Put the adverb in the correct place.
a) I have tea for breakfast. (always) b) We are late. (never) c) They play football on Saturdays. (often)

**3.** Write the times in words.
a) 9:00 b) 6:30 c) 4:15 d) 10:45

### Answers

**1.** a) I don't work on Sundays. b) We don't have lunch at twelve. c) They don't watch TV in the morning. **2.** a) I always have tea for breakfast. b) We are never late. c) They often play football on Saturdays. **3.** a) nine o'clock b) half past six c) quarter past four d) quarter to eleven.

## Unit 4 · He works, she doesn't

**Goals:** talk about jobs and about other people's routines. **Grammar:** present simple (*he, she, it*), questions with *do / does*.

![](u04.png)

### Dialogue

**Lucía:** What does your sister do, Tom?
**Tom:** She's a doctor. She works in a hospital in Leeds.
**Lucía:** Does she like her job?
**Tom:** Yes, she does. She loves it. But she works a lot: she starts at seven and she doesn't finish until six.
**Lucía:** And your brother? Where does he work?
**Tom:** He doesn't work. He studies law at university. He lives with our parents.
**Lucía:** What do you do in your free time?
**Tom:** I teach English all day, so in my free time… I don't speak!

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| job | trabajo, empleo |
| doctor / nurse | médico / enfermero |
| waiter / waitress | camarero / camarera |
| shop assistant | dependiente |
| police officer | policía |
| engineer | ingeniero |
| lawyer | abogado |
| office / factory | oficina / fábrica |
| hospital / school / shop | hospital / colegio / tienda |
| work / study | trabajar / estudiar |
| live | vivir |
| teach | enseñar |
| love / like / hate | encantar / gustar / odiar |
| a lot | mucho |
| free time | tiempo libre |

### Grammar

**1. Present simple: *he, she, it***

| Affirmative | Negative | Question | Short answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| he work**s** | he **doesn't** work | **Does** he work? | Yes, he does. / No, he doesn't. |
| she work**s** | she **doesn't** work | **Does** she work? | Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't. |

With *does* and *doesn't* the verb has no *-s*: *She doesn't work*, not «doesn't works».

**2. Spelling of the third person**

| Rule | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| + s | works, lives, likes |
| + es after s, sh, ch, x, o | watches, finishes, goes, does |
| consonant + y → ies | studies, carries |
| irregular | have → **has** |

**3. Questions: the order of the words**

Question word + *do / does* + subject + verb: *Where **does** she work?* · *What **do** you do?* · *What time **do** they start?*

### Sounds & Spelling

The third person *-s* has the same three sounds as the plural: /s/ *works, likes* · /z/ *lives, studies, goes* · /ɪz/ *watches, finishes, teaches*.

*Does* is /dʌz/ and *doesn't* is /ˈdʌznt/. *Says* is /sez/.

### Watch out!

- Do not forget the *-s*: *He work**s***, not «He work». It is the most frequent mistake at this level.
- Questions need *do* or *does*: ***Do** you like coffee?*, not «You like coffee?» or «Like you coffee?».
- *What do you do?* means «¿A qué te dedicas?». The answer is a job: *I'm a nurse.*
- *Work* is the verb and the activity. *Job* is the position: *I've got a new **job***.
- «La gente es» = *People **are***. *People* is plural.

### Exercises

**1.** Write the third person.
a) work b) go c) study d) have e) watch f) live

**2.** Complete with *do, does, don't* or *doesn't*.
a) ___ she live in Leeds? b) I ___ like my job. c) Where ___ they work? d) He ___ study on Sundays. e) ___ you speak English?

**3.** Write questions for the answers.
a) ___? He's a lawyer. b) ___? She works in a school. c) ___? Yes, I do. I love my job.

### Answers

**1.** a) works b) goes c) studies d) has e) watches f) lives. **2.** a) Does b) don't c) do d) doesn't e) Do. **3.** a) What does he do? b) Where does she work? c) Do you like your job?

## Unit 5 · Food and drink

**Goals:** order in a café, talk about what you like, ask the price. **Grammar:** countable and uncountable nouns, *some / any*, *I'd like*.

![](u05.png)

### Dialogue

**Waiter:** Good morning. What would you like?
**Lucía:** I'd like a cup of tea and a cheese sandwich, please.
**Waiter:** Milk and sugar?
**Lucía:** Some milk, please. No sugar.
**Tom:** Can I have a coffee and some toast? Have you got any orange juice?
**Waiter:** I'm sorry, we haven't got any juice today. We've got some lovely apple cake.
**Tom:** Great. A piece of cake, please. How much is that?
**Waiter:** That's nine pounds fifty, please.
**Tom:** Here you are.
**Waiter:** Thank you. Enjoy your meal!

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| tea / coffee | té / café |
| milk / sugar | leche / azúcar |
| water / juice | agua / zumo |
| bread / toast | pan / tostadas |
| butter / cheese | mantequilla / queso |
| egg | huevo |
| meat / fish / chicken | carne / pescado / pollo |
| rice / pasta | arroz / pasta |
| fruit / vegetables | fruta / verdura |
| apple / orange | manzana / naranja |
| chips / crisps | patatas fritas / patatas fritas de bolsa |
| biscuit / cake | galleta / tarta |
| a cup / a glass / a piece of | una taza / un vaso / un trozo de |
| How much is it? | ¿Cuánto es? |
| pound / pence (p) | libra / penique |

### Grammar

**1. Countable and uncountable nouns**

| | Countable | Uncountable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Examples | an apple, two eggs, three biscuits | milk, bread, rice, water, money |
| Singular and plural | yes | no plural, no *a / an* |
| To count | — | a glass **of** water, a piece **of** bread |

**2. *some* and *any***

| Sentence | We use | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative | some | We've got **some** cake. I'd like **some** biscuits. |
| Negative | any | We haven't got **any** juice. |
| Question | any | Have you got **any** eggs? |
| Offer or request | some | Would you like **some** tea? Can I have **some** water? |

**3. *I'd like* and *like***

*I'd like a coffee* (= I want one now, polite). *I like coffee* (= in general). *Would you like…?* — *Yes, please.* / *No, thank you.*

### Sounds & Spelling

Three vowels that Spanish speakers hear as «a»:

| /æ/ | /ʌ/ | /ɑː/ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| c**a**t, **a**pple, s**a**ndwich | c**u**p, b**u**tter, m**u**ch | c**ar**, gl**a**ss, h**al**f |

For /æ/ open your mouth wide. /ʌ/ is short and relaxed. /ɑː/ is long and comes from the back of the mouth.

### Watch out!

- General ideas have **no article**: *I like coffee*, not «I like the coffee». *Fish is good for you.*
- *Chips* in British English are «patatas fritas» (hot). «Patatas de bolsa» are *crisps*. In American English: *French fries* and *chips*.
- *Bread*, *toast*, *fruit*, *pasta* and *money* are uncountable: *some bread*, not «a bread» or «breads».
- *Soup* /suːp/ is «sopa». *Soap* /səʊp/ is «jabón».
- Say *please* and *thank you* often. In British English a request without *please* sounds rude.

**2.** Complete with *some* or *any*.
a) We haven't got ___ sugar. b) I'd like ___ water, please. c) Have you got ___ oranges? d) There's ___ cheese in the fridge.

**3.** Correct the mistakes.
a) I like the tea. b) I want a coffee. (make it polite) c) Can I have a bread? d) How much are it?

### Answers

**1.** a) an b) some c) a d) some e) an f) some. **2.** a) any b) some c) any d) some. **3.** a) I like tea. b) I'd like a coffee, please. c) Can I have some bread? d) How much is it?

## Unit 6 · Around town

**Goals:** say what there is in a town, ask for and give directions. **Grammar:** *there is / there are*, prepositions of place, the imperative.

![](u06.png)

### Dialogue

**Tourist:** Excuse me, is there a chemist's near here?
**Woman:** Yes, there is. There's one in Park Street. Go straight on and turn left at the traffic lights.
**Tourist:** Is it far?
**Woman:** No, it's five minutes on foot. It's next to the bank, opposite the station.
**Tourist:** And are there any cash machines?
**Woman:** There are two in the bank. But there isn't a post office in this street.
**Tourist:** Thank you very much.
**Woman:** You're welcome. Have a nice day!

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| town / city | ciudad pequeña, pueblo / ciudad |
| street / road | calle / carretera, calle |
| square | plaza |
| station | estación |
| bank | banco |
| chemist's | farmacia |
| post office | oficina de correos |
| supermarket | supermercado |
| car park | aparcamiento |
| bus stop | parada de autobús |
| traffic lights | semáforo |
| turn left / right | girar a la izquierda / derecha |
| go straight on | seguir recto |
| near / far | cerca / lejos |
| excuse me | perdone, disculpe |

### Grammar

**1. *There is* and *there are***

| | Affirmative | Negative | Question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Singular | There's a bank. | There isn't a cinema. | Is there a park? |
| Plural | There are two banks. | There aren't any shops. | Are there any hotels? |

Short answers: *Yes, there is. / No, there isn't. · Yes, there are. / No, there aren't.*

**2. Prepositions of place**

| English | Spanish | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| in | en, dentro de | The money is **in** the bank. |
| on | en, sobre | The café is **on** the corner. |
| at | en (punto) | I'm **at** the bus stop. |
| next to | al lado de | The bank is **next to** the chemist's. |
| opposite | enfrente de | It's **opposite** the station. |
| between | entre | The park is **between** the school and the river. |
| behind / in front of | detrás de / delante de | The car park is **behind** the hotel. |

**3. The imperative**

The verb with no subject: ***Go** straight on. · **Turn** right. · **Don't** turn left.*

### Sounds & Spelling

English words can begin with *s* + consonant. There is no vowel before the *s*:

*Spain* /speɪn/ · *street* /striːt/ · *station* /ˈsteɪʃn/ · *school* /skuːl/ · *square* /skweə/ · *stop* /stɒp/

Practise: make a long «sssss» and then add the word: *sssss-pain*, *sssss-treet*.

### Watch out!

- Do not say «Espain» or «estation». Start with the *s* sound.
- Spanish «en» is three words in English: ***in** London*, ***on** the table*, ***at** the station*.
- «Hay» is two forms: *there **is*** (singular) and *there **are*** (plural).
- ***Opposite*** has no preposition: *opposite the bank*, not «opposite of the bank» or «in front of» (= delante de).
- A *library* is a «biblioteca». A «librería» is a *bookshop*.

### Exercises

**1.** Complete with *is* or *are*.
a) There ___ a hotel in this street. b) There ___ two supermarkets. c) ___ there a bank near here? d) There ___ any shops.

**2.** Complete with *in, on* or *at*.
a) She lives ___ Bristol. b) The book is ___ the table. c) I'm ___ the station. d) The keys are ___ my bag.

**3.** Give directions. Translate.
a) Siga recto. b) Gire a la derecha en el semáforo. c) Está al lado del banco.

### Answers

**1.** a) is b) are c) Is d) aren't. **2.** a) in b) on c) at d) in. **3.** a) Go straight on. b) Turn right at the traffic lights. c) It's next to the bank.

## Unit 7 · What are you doing?

**Goals:** say what is happening now, describe clothes and the weather. **Grammar:** present continuous; present simple or continuous?

![](u07.png)

### Dialogue

**Emma:** Hi, Jack! It's Emma. What are you doing?
**Jack:** I'm sitting in a café in Edinburgh. I'm having a hot chocolate.
**Emma:** Lucky you! Is it raining?
**Jack:** Of course it's raining. It always rains here! I'm wearing two jumpers and a coat. What about you?
**Emma:** I'm not working today. I'm lying on the beach in Málaga. The sun is shining and I'm wearing shorts and sunglasses.
**Jack:** I don't believe you!
**Emma:** Look, I'm sending you a photo…

| --- | --- |

| coat / jacket | abrigo / chaqueta |
| jumper | jersey |
| shirt / T-shirt | camisa / camiseta |
| trousers / jeans | pantalones / vaqueros |

| sit / stand / lie | estar sentado / de pie / tumbado |

### Grammar

**1. Present continuous: *be* + verb + *-ing***

| Affirmative | Negative | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I'm working | I'm not working | Am I working? |
| you're working | you aren't working | Are you working? |
| he's / she's working | he isn't working | Is she working? |

**2. Spelling of *-ing***

| Rule | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| + ing | work → working, wear → wearing |
| ~~e~~ + ing | have → having, write → writing |
| double consonant | sit → sitting, run → running, swim → swimming |
| ie → ying | lie → lying |

**3. Present simple or present continuous?**

| Present simple | Present continuous |
| --- | --- |
| habits, things that are always true | things happening now |
| *It always rains here.* | *It's raining now.* |
| *I work in a bank.* | *I'm not working today.* |
| every day, usually, never | now, at the moment, today |

Some verbs are not normally used in the continuous: *like, love, want, know, understand, believe*. *I **don't believe** you.*

### Sounds & Spelling

The ending *-ing* is /ɪŋ/. The sound /ŋ/ comes from the back of the mouth, like the *n* in Spanish «te**n**go». Do not pronounce a hard *g* at the end: *working* /ˈwɜːkɪŋ/, *raining* /ˈreɪnɪŋ/, *sitting* /ˈsɪtɪŋ/.

### Watch out!

- Spanish often uses the simple present for now. English does not: «¿Qué haces?» (now) = *What **are** you **doing**?*, not «What do you do?» (= your job).
- The weather needs *it*: ***It's** raining*, not «Is raining».
- «Hace calor» = *It**'s** hot*, with *be*, not «It makes hot».
- *Trousers*, *jeans* and *shorts* are always plural: *My trousers **are** blue.* In American English *trousers* are *pants*; in British English *pants* are underwear.
- *Wear* is for clothes (*I'm wearing a coat*). *Carry* is for things in your hands.

### Exercises

**1.** Write the *-ing* form.
a) wear b) have c) sit d) rain e) lie f) swim

**2.** Complete with the present simple or continuous.
a) Look! It ___ (snow). b) She usually ___ (wear) jeans. c) I ___ (not work) today. d) ___ you ___ (like) this jumper? e) What ___ you ___ (do) at the moment?

**3.** Correct the mistakes.
a) Is raining. b) I'm wear a coat. c) What do you do now? d) My trousers is new.

### Answers

**1.** a) wearing b) having c) sitting d) raining e) lying f) swimming. **2.** a) 's snowing b) wears c) 'm not working d) Do … like e) are … doing. **3.** a) It's raining. b) I'm wearing a coat. c) What are you doing now? d) My trousers are new.

## Unit 8 · Can you swim?

**Goals:** talk about abilities, hobbies and what you like doing. **Grammar:** *can / can't*, *like + -ing*, object pronouns.

![](u08.png)

### Dialogue

**Tom:** What do you do at the weekend, Lucía?
**Lucía:** I love dancing, and I like going to the cinema with my friends. And you?
**Tom:** I play tennis every Saturday. Can you play?
**Lucía:** No, I can't. I'm terrible at sports. But I can play the guitar.
**Tom:** Really? I can't play any instruments. Can you teach me?
**Lucía:** Of course! Can you sing?
**Tom:** I can sing… but my friends hate it. They always tell me to stop!

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| swim / run | nadar / correr |
| dance / sing | bailar / cantar |
| play football / tennis | jugar al fútbol / al tenis |
| play the guitar / the piano | tocar la guitarra / el piano |
| ride a bike | montar en bici |
| drive | conducir |
| read / draw | leer / dibujar |
| go to the cinema | ir al cine |
| listen to music | escuchar música |
| go shopping | ir de compras |
| at the weekend | el fin de semana |
| good at / bad at | bueno en / malo en |
| really? | ¿de verdad? |
| of course | por supuesto |

### Grammar

**1. *can* and *can't***

*Can* is the same for all persons. It has no *-s* and no *do*.

| Affirmative | Negative | Question | Short answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I can swim. | I can't swim. | Can you swim? | Yes, I can. / No, I can't. |
| She can drive. | She can't drive. | Can she drive? | Yes, she can. / No, she can't. |

After *can* the verb has no *to*: *I can **swim***, not «I can to swim».

**2. *like, love, hate* + *-ing***

*I **love** danc**ing**. · She **likes** read**ing**. · We **don't like** gett**ing** up early. · He **hates** cook**ing**.*

**3. Object pronouns**

| Subject | I | you | he | she | it | we | they |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Object | me | you | him | her | it | us | them |

They go **after** the verb or a preposition: *Can you teach **me**? · I love **them**. · Listen to **her**.*

### Sounds & Spelling

*Can* in a sentence is weak: /kən/. *I can swim* /aɪ kən ˈswɪm/. *Can't* is strong and long in British English: /kɑːnt/. The difference is the vowel, not the *t*.

At the end of a sentence *can* is strong: *Yes, I can* /kæn/.

### Watch out!

- «Me gusta bailar» = *I like dancing*. The person is the subject in English: ***I** like*, not «Me likes» or «It likes me».
- «Sé nadar» = *I **can** swim*, not «I know swim».
- «Tocar» an instrument is *play*, with *the*: *play **the** guitar*. Sports have no article: *play tennis*.
- The object pronoun goes after the verb: *I love **it***, not «I it love».
- *At the weekend* is British. Americans say *on the weekend*.

### Exercises

**1.** Complete with *can* or *can't*.
a) I ___ swim, but I ___ dive. b) ___ you drive? — No, I ___. c) She ___ speak three languages: English, Spanish and French.

**2.** Complete with the object pronoun.
a) I love my friends. I see ___ every weekend. b) This is Emma. Do you know ___? c) We're here. Can you see ___? d) I like this song. Listen to ___.

**3.** Write sentences.
a) I / love / read b) she / hate / cook c) we / not like / get up early

### Answers

**1.** a) can, can't b) Can, can't c) can. **2.** a) them b) her c) us d) it. **3.** a) I love reading. b) She hates cooking. c) We don't like getting up early.

## Unit 9 · Last weekend

**Goals:** talk about your weekend and about the past. **Grammar:** *was / were*, past simple of regular verbs and ten irregular verbs.

![](u09.png)

### Dialogue

**Emma:** Hi, Jack. How was your weekend?
**Jack:** It was great, thanks. I went to Brighton with some friends.
**Emma:** Lovely! Was the weather good?
**Jack:** It wasn't bad. It rained on Saturday, so we visited a museum and had fish and chips. On Sunday it was sunny and we walked on the beach.
**Emma:** Did you swim?
**Jack:** No, we didn't. The water was freezing! What about you?
**Emma:** I stayed at home. I watched three films and I didn't go out. I was very tired.

### Words

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| yesterday | ayer |
| last night / last week | anoche / la semana pasada |
| two days ago | hace dos días |
| the weekend | el fin de semana |
| visit | visitar |
| walk | caminar, pasear |
| stay at home | quedarse en casa |
| go out | salir |
| beach / museum | playa / museo |
| film | película |
| tired | cansado |
| great / terrible | genial / horrible |
| freezing | helado |
| so | así que |

### Grammar

**1. Past simple of *be***

| Affirmative | Negative | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I / he / she / it **was** | I **wasn't** | **Was** it good? |
| you / we / they **were** | we **weren't** | **Were** you tired? |

**2. Past simple: regular verbs**

Verb + *-ed*, the same for all persons: *I walk**ed**, she watch**ed**, they visit**ed***.

| Rule | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| + ed | walked, watched, rained |
| + d | lived, liked, danced |
| consonant + y → ied | studied, carried |
| double consonant | stopped, planned |

**3. Ten irregular verbs**

| Verb | Past | Verb | Past |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| go | went | see | saw |
| have | had | come | came |
| do | did | get | got |
| eat | ate | make | made |
| buy | bought | say | said |

**4. Negatives and questions**

*did / didn't* + verb (no *-ed*): *I **didn't go** out. · **Did** you **swim**?* — *Yes, I did. / No, I didn't.*

### Sounds & Spelling

The ending *-ed* has three sounds. It is a new syllable only after *t* and *d*.

| /t/ | /d/ | /ɪd/ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| walked, watched, stopped | rained, lived, stayed | visited, wanted, needed |

*Walked* has **one** syllable: /wɔːkt/, not «wal-ked».

### Watch out!

- «Hace dos días» = *two days **ago***. *Ago* goes after the time.
- After *did* the verb is in the base form: *Did you **go**?*, not «Did you went?».
- The past simple is the same for all persons: *he walked*, not «he walkeds».
- «El sábado» = ***on** Saturday*. «El fin de semana pasado» = ***last** weekend*, with no article and no preposition.
- *Film* is British. Americans say *movie*.

### Exercises

**1.** Complete with *was, were, wasn't* or *weren't*.
a) The film ___ great. b) We ___ at home yesterday; we were in Brighton. c) ___ you tired? d) It ___ cold, it was freezing!

**2.** Write the past simple.
a) walk b) go c) study d) have e) stop f) see g) buy h) live

**3.** Make questions and negatives.
a) She visited a museum. (?) b) They went out. (–) c) You saw the film. (?)

### Answers

**1.** a) was b) weren't c) Were d) wasn't. **2.** a) walked b) went c) studied d) had e) stopped f) saw g) bought h) lived. **3.** a) Did she visit a museum? b) They didn't go out. c) Did you see the film?
